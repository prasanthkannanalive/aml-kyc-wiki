## 4.4 Filing SARs and STRs on virtual-asset activity

Suspicious activity reporting for crypto follows the same legal framework as traditional AML — but the evidence is different and the narrative must explain technical concepts to non-technical FIU analysts.

### When to file

The legal standard is the same: **suspicion** — a reasonable belief that activity may involve money laundering, terrorist financing or other financial crime. For crypto specifically:

- Transactions involving sanctioned entities or jurisdictions
- Patterns consistent with layering, structuring or placement
- Customer inability or unwillingness to explain source of funds
- Interaction with darknet markets, mixers or ransomware addresses
- Behavior inconsistent with stated business purpose
- Travel Rule data inconsistencies or failures

### What the SAR must contain

**Subject information** — customer identity, account details, wallet addresses, transaction hashes. For crypto, include:

- All relevant wallet addresses (deposits, withdrawals, internal transfers)
- Transaction hashes for key transactions
- Chain-analytics risk scores and exposure categories
- Exchange deposit addresses if funds moved through other VASPs

**Transaction details** — dates, amounts, currencies, counterparties. For crypto:

- Specify the virtual asset type (BTC, ETH, USDT, etc.)
- Include fiat equivalent values where available
- Note the blockchain(s) involved
- Describe the transaction pattern, not just individual transactions

**Narrative** — the most important part. Must explain:

- What the customer was doing and why it's suspicious
- The technical evidence (risk scores, exposure categories, transaction patterns)
- What the customer claimed vs. what the evidence shows
- Why you believe it's suspicious, not just unusual
- What action you took (account restriction, EDD request, etc.)

### Writing for the FIU

FIU analysts may not understand crypto. The narrative must:

- **Explain technical terms** — "the customer deposited 14.7 BTC from an address with 72% exposure to Tornado Cash mixer and darknet market sources"
- **Use analogies** — "equivalent to depositing cash from a known drug market into a bank account"
- **Focus on the red flags** — not the technology, but why the pattern is suspicious
- **Include the customer context** — who they claim to be, what they claimed the funds were, why that's inconsistent

### Common mistakes

- **Too technical** — narratives full of blockchain jargon that FIU analysts can't act on
- **Too vague** — "suspicious crypto activity" without specifics
- **Missing context** — reporting the transaction without explaining why it's suspicious
- **No customer perspective** — not including what the customer claimed or how they responded to inquiries
- **Ignoring Travel Rule data** — failing to mention whether counterparty information was collected and verified

### Recordkeeping

VASPs must retain SAR records for the period required by their jurisdiction (typically 5 years). For crypto, this includes:

- Transaction hashes and blockchain data
- Chain-analytics reports and risk scores
- Travel Rule messages sent and received
- Customer communications and EDD documentation
- Analyst workpapers and decision rationale
