## 4.3 Transaction monitoring rules for crypto

Crypto transaction monitoring differs from traditional AML monitoring in data availability, velocity and typology. This chapter covers the rule categories that matter.

### Deposit-side rules

**High-risk source screening** — every deposit address is screened against chain-analytics databases. Rules trigger on:

- Direct exposure to sanctioned entities (auto-freeze)
- Indirect exposure above threshold (alert for review)
- Interaction with mixers, darknet markets, ransomware addresses (alert)
- First-time interaction with high-risk service categories (alert)

**Velocity and volume rules** — same logic as traditional AML but calibrated for crypto's higher velocity:

- Large deposits relative to customer profile
- Rapid deposit-withdrawal cycles (in-and-out pattern)
- Multiple deposits from different high-risk sources in short period
- Deposit amounts just below reporting thresholds (structuring)

**Geographic rules** — deposits from wallets associated with sanctioned jurisdictions or high-risk corridors.

### Withdrawal-side rules

**Travel Rule compliance** — verify that required counterparty data was collected and transmitted for withdrawals above threshold.

**Destination screening** — screen withdrawal addresses against sanctions lists and high-risk service databases. A withdrawal to a sanctioned entity's known wallet is an automatic block.

**Unhosted wallet scrutiny** — withdrawals to unhosted wallets require enhanced verification: proof of wallet ownership, source of funds documentation, or enhanced due diligence on the customer.

**Pattern rules** — withdrawals that match layering patterns: rapid movement through multiple addresses, consolidation-then-distribution, peel chain structures.

### Behavioral rules

**Customer baseline deviation** — a customer who historically trades small amounts suddenly deposits $500k triggers review.

**Time-pattern analysis** — transactions at unusual hours, rapid-fire transactions, or patterns matching known scam typologies.

**Counterparty diversity** — a customer who suddenly interacts with dozens of new addresses or exchanges.

### Alert tuning

Crypto monitoring generates high alert volumes because:

- Every transaction is public and traceable — more data, more signals
- Crypto users are more sophisticated — they know how to structure transactions
- The threat landscape evolves rapidly — new mixers, scams and sanctions designations appear constantly

Effective tuning requires:

- **Regular rule review** — quarterly at minimum, more often for high-risk products
- **False-positive analysis** — track alert outcomes, tune thresholds to reduce noise without missing signal
- **Customer segmentation** — different rules for retail vs. institutional, low-risk vs. high-risk
- **Feedback loops** — analyst dispositions feed back into rule tuning; confirmed cases validate rules, false positives reveal gaps
