## 1.4 Licensing regimes: MiCA, the US, UK and the Travel Rule

FATF sets the global floor; each major jurisdiction implements it differently. Understanding the patchwork matters because a VASP's compliance obligations depend on where it is incorporated, where its customers are, and where its servers sit.

### European Union — MiCA

The **Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA)** is the most comprehensive crypto-specific regulatory framework to date. It entered into force in 2023 with phased application through 2024–2025.

MiCA creates three categories of regulated entity:

- **CASPs (Crypto-Asset Service Providers)** — the EU equivalent of VASPs, covering exchange, custody, trading platform operation, advice and portfolio management
- **Issuers of asset-referenced tokens (ARTs)** and **e-money tokens (EMTs)** — stablecoins fall here; issuers face reserve, redemption and disclosure requirements
- **Crypto-asset service providers** face conduct, governance, prudential and AML obligations flowing from existing financial-services legislation

MiCA's innovation is **passporting**: a CASP licensed in one EU member state can serve customers across the entire Union. For compliance teams this means one licence, twenty-seven markets — and one AML framework.

### United States — the patchwork

The US has no single crypto law. Regulation is split across agencies:

- **FinCEN** treats convertible virtual currency as money transmission under the Bank Secrecy Act — exchanges and custodial wallet providers are money services businesses (MSBs) required to register, maintain AML programmes and file SARs
- **SEC** regulates crypto assets that are securities (the Howey test determines this — most tokens launched through ICOs are)
- **CFTC** regulates crypto derivatives and commodities (Bitcoin and Ethereum are commodities under its jurisdiction)
- **State regulators** — New York's BitLicense, state money transmitter laws — layer additional requirements

The result is overlapping, sometimes contradictory obligations. A US crypto exchange may need FinCEN MSB registration, state money transmitter licences in dozens of states, SEC registration if it lists securities, and CFTC registration for derivatives.

### United Kingdom — post-Brexit alignment

The UK's **Financial Services and Markets Act 2023** brought cryptoassets within the regulatory perimeter for the first time. The FCA now regulates cryptoasset firms for AML purposes — registration is required for any business carrying on cryptoasset business in the UK, including overseas firms serving UK customers.

The UK also implements the Travel Rule through its **Cryptoasset Financial Promotion regime** and FCA rules, with thresholds aligned to the FATF standard.

### The Travel Rule in practice

Implementation remains the hardest operational problem in crypto compliance:

- **Messaging standards** — the Travel Rule requires structured data exchange, but the crypto ecosystem lacks a universal messaging protocol. IVMS 101 is the emerging standard, but adoption is incomplete
- **Unhosted wallets** — most blockchain transactions never touch two VASPs simultaneously, so there is no counterparty to exchange data with
- **Thresholds differ** — USD 1,000 in the US and EU, GBP 1,000 in the UK, but other jurisdictions vary; a transaction might trigger the rule in one country and not another
- **Verification asymmetry** — the sending VASP knows its customer; the receiving VASP may have no idea who the originator is
