## 5.2 Cross-border requests: MLATs and chain-data sharing

Crypto crime is inherently cross-border, and investigations require coordination across jurisdictions. The legal mechanisms are the same as traditional MLATs, but the data is different.

### Mutual Legal Assistance Treaties (MLATs)

MLATs are bilateral agreements between countries for sharing evidence in criminal investigations. For crypto cases, requests typically seek:

- **Exchange customer data** — KYC records, transaction histories, login logs, IP addresses
- **Wallet attribution data** — chain-analytics reports, clustering analysis, exposure scores
- **Travel Rule messages** — originator and beneficiary information exchanged between VASPs
- **Blockchain data** — while the ledger itself is public, requests may seek specific transaction analyses or vendor reports

MLATs are slow — typically months to years — which is why crypto investigations often rely on **faster mechanisms**.

### Faster channels

**Egmont Group** — FIU-to-FIU information exchange for suspicious activity reports. Faster than MLATs but limited to financial intelligence, not evidence.

**INTERPOL and Europol** — operational coordination, joint investigations, and real-time information sharing. Europol's EC3 (European Cybercrime Centre) specializes in crypto crime.

**Bilateral agreements** — some countries have expedited MLATs or police-to-police cooperation agreements for urgent matters.

**Chain-analytics vendor data sharing** — vendors like Chainalysis and TRM Labs maintain shared intelligence databases that law enforcement can access through partnership agreements, bypassing the need for MLATs for attribution data.

### The jurisdictional challenge

Crypto's borderlessness creates jurisdictional gaps:

- **Non-cooperative jurisdictions** — exchanges and services incorporated in countries that do not honor MLATs or have weak rule of law
- **Decentralized infrastructure** — DeFi protocols, bridges and unhosted wallets have no legal entity to serve process on
- **Privacy coins** — Monero transactions cannot be traced even with international cooperation
- **Mixer contracts** — sanctioned smart contracts (like Tornado Cash) cannot be "shut down" in the traditional sense; they exist as long as the Ethereum network does

### Practical investigation strategies

**Follow the fiat** — the ultimate cash-out point is usually a regulated exchange or payment processor that can be served with legal process.

**Exploit KYC gaps** — even pseudonymous users leave traces at off-ramps; exchanges that failed to collect adequate KYC may have weaker data but still provide transaction records.

**Use vendor intelligence** — chain-analytics vendors often have attribution data that individual agencies cannot replicate; partnership agreements provide faster access than MLATs.

**Target infrastructure** — rather than chasing individual users, target the services they rely on: exchanges, mixers, payment processors, and fiat on-ramps.
