## 3.4 Reconstructing a case end to end

This chapter walks through a complete tracing engagement — the kind a compliance analyst, investigator or chain-analytics specialist would actually perform. It integrates everything from Modules 2 and 3.

### The scenario

A VASP's monitoring system flags a customer deposit: 14.7 BTC (~$900,000) arriving from an unattributed address. The customer claims it is proceeds from a legitimate OTC crypto trading business. The deposit address's history shows no prior transactions — it was freshly generated.

### Step 1 — Isolate the deposit

Pull the transaction details from the blockchain explorer:

- **Transaction hash** — the unique identifier
- **Input addresses** — where the funds came from (possibly multiple inputs if the sender consolidated UTXOs)
- **Output addresses** — where the funds went (the deposit address plus any change back to sender)
- **Timestamp and confirmations**

Run the deposit address through a chain-analytics tool. The risk score shows **72/100 — high risk**, with exposure categories: mixer (Tornado Cash), darknet market, and ransomware.

### Step 2 — Trace backwards

Follow the inputs upstream:

- Input 1: 10 BTC from address `1A3x...` — this address received funds from a Tornado Cash withdrawal 6 hours earlier
- Input 2: 4.7 BTC from address `bc1q...` — this address received funds from a known darknet market wallet 2 days earlier

The pattern is clear: the sender is consolidating funds from high-risk sources into a fresh address and depositing to the VASP. This is a classic **layering-then-cashing-out** pattern.

### Step 3 — Trace forwards

Look at what happened after the deposit:

- The VASP credited the customer's account
- 2 days later, the customer withdrew 10 BTC to an external wallet
- That external wallet sent 8 BTC to a different exchange deposit address 12 hours later

The second exchange is also a VASP — the funds are moving through multiple off-ramps, possibly to obscure the ultimate destination.

### Step 4 — Attribution and context

Check the customer's profile:

- Onboarded 3 months ago with minimal documentation
- Claimed occupation: "cryptocurrency trader"
- No prior transaction history before this deposit
- Source of funds declaration: "trading profits" — but no evidence of prior trading activity
- IP address geolocates to a sanctioned jurisdiction

The customer risk rating was **medium** — should have been **high** given the jurisdiction and occupation.

### Step 5 — Decision

The evidence supports:

- **Enhanced due diligence** — request source of funds documentation, proof of trading activity, and explanation for the high-risk deposit sources
- **Consider SAR filing** — the pattern is consistent with money laundering: layering through mixers and darknet markets, then cashing out through VASPs
- **Consider account restriction** — if the customer cannot provide satisfactory explanation, restrict withdrawals pending investigation

### What the case file should contain

- Transaction hashes and graph visualization
- Chain-analytics risk scores and exposure categories
- Customer profile and onboarding documentation
- Source-of-funds declarations
- Timeline of deposits, withdrawals and transfers
- Analyst narrative explaining the pattern and conclusion
- Recommended action (EDD, SAR, restriction, or closure)

This is the workflow every crypto compliance analyst must be able to execute — and the reason chain-analytics tools exist.
