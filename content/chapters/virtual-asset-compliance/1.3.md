## 1.3 FATF Recommendations 15–16 and the VASP definition

The FATF is the global standard-setter for AML/CFT, and its approach to virtual assets has evolved through three phases: initial guidance (2014), full recommendations (2019), and updated guidance (2021, revised 2024). The core architecture is Recommendations 15 and 16.

### Recommendation 15 — Virtual Asset Service Providers

R.15 requires countries to regulate **virtual asset service providers (VASPs)** — businesses that conduct one or more of these activities for or on behalf of customers:

- exchange between virtual assets and fiat currencies
- exchange between one or more forms of virtual assets
- transfer of virtual assets
- safekeeping or administration of virtual assets or instruments enabling control over them
- participation in and provision of financial services related to an issuer's offer or sale of virtual assets

The critical expansion: FATF's 2019 revision extended obligations to **VASPs offering services outside their home jurisdiction** — closing the loophole where a platform incorporated in a low-regulation country served customers everywhere else.

### The Travel Rule — Recommendation 16

R.16 is the operational heart of crypto AML. It requires VASPs (and traditional financial institutions) to **exchange originator and beneficiary information** when transferring virtual assets above a threshold — typically USD/EUR 1,000 or 3,000, depending on the jurisdiction.

For a wire transfer, the originator's name, account number and address travel with the payment. For a Bitcoin transaction, nothing travels with it — just addresses and amounts. The Travel Rule is the mechanism that forces VASPs to attach the missing identity data when they custody both ends of a transfer.

The problem: **most crypto transfers never touch a VASP**. A user withdrawing to a self-hosted wallet and sending directly to another self-hosted wallet bypasses the Travel Rule entirely. This is why FATF's 2021 guidance introduced the concept of **unhosted wallets** — wallets not controlled by a VASP — and recommended that VASPs collect and retain information about them when transacting above thresholds. Implementation varies wildly by jurisdiction.

### What FATF actually requires of VASPs

The standard AML toolkit applies in full:

- **Customer due diligence** — identify and verify customers, including beneficial owners of legal entities
- **Suspicious activity reporting** — file STRs/SARs on suspicious transactions
- **Record keeping** — retain transaction and identity data
- **Sanctions screening** — screen customers and transactions against sanctions lists
- **Risk assessment** — assess ML/TF risks and apply mitigation proportionate to the risk

### The 2024 targeted update

FATF's June 2024 targeted update assessed global implementation and found it **uneven**. Some jurisdictions have robust VASP licensing regimes; others have minimal or no regulation. The update flagged particular concerns about:

- VASPs operating without licences in high-risk jurisdictions
- Weak Travel Rule implementation, especially for transfers involving unhosted wallets
- DeFi arrangements that evade the VASP definition entirely
- Stablecoins issued by non-bank entities with limited AML oversight
