## 5.1 Seizures, forfeiture and exchange cooperation

When crypto crime is detected, the recovery phase involves legal processes that differ from traditional asset recovery — and the technical capabilities of exchanges are central.

### How seizures work

**Exchange asset freezes** — the most common seizure method. Law enforcement serves a legal order on the exchange, which freezes the target's account and prevents withdrawals. The exchange holds the private keys to custodial wallets, making this technically straightforward.

**On-chain freezes** — for non-custodial assets, authorities can sometimes freeze at the protocol level (e.g., freezing USDC through Circle's blacklist function) or through exchange cooperation when the funds reach a custodial endpoint.

**Private key seizure** — in rare cases, authorities seize devices containing private keys or compel password disclosure. This is technically difficult but has been done in major cases.

### The forfeiture process

After seizure, assets typically go through:

1. **Forfeiture proceedings** — civil or criminal process to establish government ownership
2. **Liquidation** — seized crypto is often sold at auction or through designated exchanges to convert to fiat for victim restitution or government use
3. **Victim restitution** — in fraud cases, recovered funds may be returned to victims through court-ordered distribution

### Exchange cooperation

VASPs are critical partners in crypto investigations:

**Data provision** — exchanges hold KYC data, login logs, IP addresses, device fingerprints, travel rule messages and transaction histories. Law enforcement requests this through legal process (subpoenas, MLATs, court orders).

**Technical assistance** — exchanges can sometimes trace funds through their own systems, identify related accounts, and flag suspicious activity in real-time.

**Asset recovery** — exchanges can freeze and transfer seized assets to government-controlled wallets.

**Cooperation levels** — major exchanges have dedicated law-enforcement response teams and cooperate proactively. Smaller exchanges and offshore platforms may be slower or less cooperative, especially if operating in non-cooperative jurisdictions.

### Notable cases

- **Colonial Pipeline (2021)** — ransomware payment of 75 BTC; FBI recovered ~64 BTC by obtaining the private key through investigation
- **Hydra darknet market (2022)** — German authorities seized ~2 billion USD in crypto, demonstrating that even privacy-focused markets can be dismantled
- **Lazarus Group (ongoing)** — multiple exchange freezes and seizures targeting North Korean hacking proceeds, showing that even state-sponsored actors leave recoverable trails
- **Tornado Cash sanctions (2022)** — OFAC sanctioned the mixer contract; subsequent enforcement actions showed the limits and possibilities of on-chain sanctions enforcement
