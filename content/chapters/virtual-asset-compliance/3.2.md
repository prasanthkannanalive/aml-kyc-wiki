## 3.2 Exposure scoring and risk ratings

The risk score is the interface between chain-analytics and compliance operations. Understanding what goes into it — and what it misses — determines whether your alerting is useful or noise.

### How scores are calculated

Vendor risk scores typically combine several factors:

**Direct exposure** — does the address or transaction interact directly with a known high-risk service? A deposit to a sanctioned mixer is severe; a transaction through Tornado Cash is flagged automatically.

**Indirect exposure** — what percentage of the funds' history traces back to high-risk sources? If 30% of a customer's deposit originated from darknet market wallets five hops ago, the indirect exposure is material even if the immediate transaction looks clean.

**Proximity scoring** — how many hops separate the address from a high-risk entity? Direct interaction is severe; three hops removed is moderate; ten hops is minimal. Vendors use graph-distance algorithms to weight proximity.

**Volume and velocity** — large amounts moving quickly through high-risk corridors score higher than small amounts moving slowly.

**Counterparty diversity** — addresses interacting with many distinct high-risk services score higher than those touching one.

### The categories that matter

Chain-analytics vendors categorize exposure to specific threat types:

- **Sanctioned entities** — OFAC, UN, EU and UK designated addresses and entities
- **Darknet markets** — AlphaBay, Hansa, Hydra and successors
- **Mixers** — Tornado Cash, Blender.io, ChipMixer and others
- **Ransomware** — addresses identified in ransomware payment notes
- **Scams and fraud** — addresses from pig-butchering, investment scams, romance scams
- **Gambling** — unlicensed or high-risk gambling services
- **Sanctions-adjacent** — exchanges and services in sanctioned jurisdictions

### Using scores in practice

A VASP's monitoring system typically applies rules like:

- **Auto-freeze** — direct exposure to OFAC-sanctioned addresses above a threshold
- **Enhanced due diligence** — indirect exposure above X% triggers a manual review
- **Alert generation** — moderate exposure creates an alert for analyst review
- **Customer risk rating** — aggregate exposure across a customer's history feeds their overall risk score

The key operational question is **thresholds**. Too low and you drown in false positives; too high and you miss real exposure. Vendors provide recommended thresholds, but each institution must calibrate against its own risk appetite, customer base and regulatory expectations.

### What scores cannot tell you

A risk score answers "how much of this address's history touches high-risk services?" It does **not** answer:

- Whether the customer is complicit or a victim (a ransomware victim who paid the ransom has the same exposure as the attacker)
- Whether the exposure is recent or historical (a customer who used a mixer in 2021 but has been clean since may deserve different treatment than one who used it yesterday)
- Whether the customer knew (an exchange customer depositing stolen funds unknowingly looks identical to one laundering deliberately)

This is why scores trigger **review**, not automatic action — except where law requires it (sanctions).
