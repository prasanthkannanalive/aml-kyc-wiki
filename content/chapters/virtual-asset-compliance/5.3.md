## 5.3 Typologies: ransomware, scams, darknet markets and sanctions evasion

This chapter covers the major crypto crime typologies that compliance analysts will encounter — and the detection signatures for each.

### Ransomware

**Pattern** — malware encrypts victim data; attacker demands payment in Bitcoin or Monero, typically with a deadline.

**Detection signatures**:
- Deposits from addresses matching known ransomware payment notes
- Rapid movement after deposit (ransom payments are often moved quickly to avoid tracing)
- Interaction with mixers immediately after receipt
- Payments in privacy coins (Monero) — high-risk indicator

**Compliance response** — screen deposit addresses against ransomware databases; flag customers who receive ransomware payments; monitor for rapid movement patterns.

### Scams and fraud

**Pig-butchering** — long-con social engineering scams where victims are groomed over weeks or months, then convinced to "invest" in fake crypto platforms. The scammer controls the platform and steals deposits.

**Romance scams** — similar grooming but focused on emotional manipulation; victims send crypto directly to scammer wallets.

**Investment scams** — fake trading platforms, Ponzi schemes, "guaranteed returns" offers. Victims deposit crypto that is immediately moved to scammer wallets.

**Detection signatures**:
- Deposits from addresses associated with known scam operations
- Victim profiles: older customers, unusual transaction patterns, deposits matching scam typologies
- Rapid movement of deposited funds to high-risk destinations
- Interaction with unhosted wallets immediately after deposit

### Darknet markets

**Pattern** — illegal goods and services sold on Tor-hidden marketplaces; payments in Bitcoin or Monero.

**Detection signatures**:
- Direct interaction with known market wallet addresses
- Structured payments matching market pricing
- Use of privacy coins or mixers immediately before or after market transactions
- Customer profiles consistent with drug purchasing (small regular payments, specific timing patterns)

### Sanctions evasion

**Pattern** — sanctioned entities and jurisdictions using crypto to move value outside the reach of traditional financial controls.

**Detection signatures**:
- Direct interaction with OFAC/UN/EU sanctioned addresses
- Use of mixers to obscure origin before cashing out
- Interaction with exchanges in sanctioned jurisdictions
- Structuring transactions to avoid Travel Rule thresholds
- Use of privacy coins or chain-hopping to break the trail

### Money laundering through crypto

**Placement** — depositing illicit fiat into crypto via unregulated on-ramps or P2P platforms.

**Layering** — moving through mixers, chain-hopping, DeFi protocols, and multiple wallets to break the trail.

**Integration** — cashing out through exchanges, payment processors, or merchant services to appear legitimate.

### The detection challenge

Crypto typologies are harder to detect than traditional AML because:

- Transactions are pseudonymous — no names, only addresses
- Velocity is higher — criminals can move funds faster than compliance can react
- Techniques evolve rapidly — new mixers, privacy coins and DeFi protocols emerge constantly
- Jurisdictional gaps — services in non-cooperative jurisdictions operate with impunity

Effective detection requires **proactive monitoring** (real-time screening, behavioral analytics), **threat intelligence** (chain-analytics databases, vendor updates), and **collaboration** (law-enforcement partnerships, industry information sharing).
