## 2.4 Mixers, chain-hopping and privacy techniques

Criminals know that the public ledger exposes everything. Their countermeasures define the cat-and-mouse game of on-chain compliance — and each technique has a detection signature.

### Mixers and tumblers

A **mixer** (or tumbler) breaks the link between inputs and outputs by pooling funds from many users and redistributing them to new addresses. The user sends crypto in, waits, and receives a roughly equivalent amount out — minus a fee — at a different address with no obvious connection.

**Centralized mixers** — services like Tornado Cash (sanctioned by OFAC in 2022) that operate as smart contracts or custodial services. They are the easiest to detect because they have known contract addresses and characteristic usage patterns.

**Decentralized mixers** — protocols where the mixing happens through smart contracts without a central operator. Harder to shut down, harder to attribute, but still detectable through contract interaction patterns.

Detection signatures: deposits to known mixer contracts, characteristic equal-amount outputs, timing patterns, and the use of privacy coins immediately after mixing.

### Chain-hopping

**Chain-hopping** moves value across multiple blockchains to break the trail. The pattern:

1. Send Bitcoin to a cross-chain bridge or swap service
2. Receive an equivalent amount in a different asset on a different chain (e.g., Bitcoin → Ethereum USDC)
3. Move through DeFi protocols, mixers or additional swaps
4. Cash out through a different exchange or off-ramp

Each hop adds analytical friction. A Bitcoin-to-Ethereum bridge creates a record on both chains, but correlating them requires bridge-specific data. Chain-analytics vendors maintain bridge datasets, but coverage is incomplete — especially for smaller bridges and decentralized exchanges.

### Privacy coins

**Monero, Zcash and similar** are designed to obscure transaction details:

- **Monero** uses ring signatures (hiding which input is the real one among decoys), stealth addresses (hiding the recipient) and RingCT (hiding the amount). It is genuinely private — even chain-analytics firms admit they cannot trace Monero transactions reliably.
- **Zcash** offers optional privacy through zero-knowledge proofs (zk-SNARKs). Transparent transactions are traceable; shielded transactions are not.
- **Bitcoin privacy improvements** — CoinJoin, PayJoin and Taproot-based techniques offer partial privacy without leaving the Bitcoin ecosystem.

For compliance, privacy coins are the hardest problem: transactions are visible but unintelligible. Many regulators and VASPs have responded by **delisting** privacy coins entirely — a blunt but effective control.

### Peel chains and peeling services

A **peel chain** is a pattern where a large UTXO is repeatedly split: each transaction sends most of the value forward and "peels off" a small amount to a new address. This is how darknet markets and ransomware operators structure payouts — creating many small outputs that each go to a different destination, making it harder to identify the ultimate beneficiary.

### The compliance reality

No technique is undetectable in principle — but each raises the cost of analysis. The practical approach for VASPs is **risk-based**: flag interactions with mixers, privacy coins and high-risk bridges; apply enhanced due diligence to customers whose transaction histories show these patterns; and treat direct exposure to sanctioned mixers (like Tornado Cash) as an automatic escalation.
