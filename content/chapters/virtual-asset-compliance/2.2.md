## 2.2 UTXO versus account models

The two dominant blockchain designs handle transactions differently, and the difference shapes what compliance analysts see.

### UTXO — Bitcoin's model

**Unspent Transaction Output (UTXO)** is the model Bitcoin uses. Every transaction consumes previous outputs and creates new ones. Think of it like cash: you have a wallet full of bills and coins of various denominations; to pay someone you hand over specific bills and receive change.

In UTXO terms:

- Your "balance" is the sum of all UTXOs addressed to you
- A transaction selects specific UTXOs as inputs and creates new UTXOs as outputs
- If inputs exceed the payment, the difference returns as a **change output** to a new address you control
- Each UTXO can only be spent once — it is either spent or unspent

The compliance implication: **change outputs create address proliferation**. One user making one payment may generate two new addresses — the payment destination and the change address. Over time, one person controls hundreds of addresses. Clustering heuristics (Chapter 2.3) exist partly to solve this.

### Account model — Ethereum's approach

Ethereum uses an **account model** closer to traditional banking. Each address has a persistent state — balance, nonce, contract code — and transactions directly debit one account and credit another.

In account terms:

- Your balance is a stored value on the account
- A transaction specifies a single sender and recipient
- No change outputs — the balance updates directly
- Smart contracts are accounts too, with code that executes on every transaction

The compliance implication: account models are **easier to analyse at the address level** because one address = one persistent entity. But they enable **smart contracts**, which create complex programmable behaviour — DeFi protocols, automated market makers, mixer contracts — that UTXO chains cannot express. Chapter 5.4 covers why this matters.

### Sidechains, layer-2 and bridges

Both models now operate across **layer-2 networks** (Lightning for Bitcoin, rollups for Ethereum) and **sidechains**. Value moves between layers through **bridges** — contracts or protocols that lock assets on one chain and mint representations on another. For compliance, bridges are critical because they are the primary **chain-hopping** mechanism: moving value across chains specifically to break the analytical trail.
