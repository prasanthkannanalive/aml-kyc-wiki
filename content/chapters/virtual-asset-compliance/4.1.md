## 4.1 Onboarding and CDD for crypto customers

Virtual-asset customer due diligence follows the same risk-based logic as traditional AML, but the risk factors and verification methods differ.

### Risk factors specific to crypto

**Product risk** — custody vs. non-custody, trading vs. staking, fiat on-ramp vs. crypto-to-crypto. Custodial services that hold customer assets carry higher risk than pure exchange interfaces.

**Customer risk** — same as traditional AML: PEPs, adverse media, sanctions exposure, high-risk jurisdictions. Crypto adds: anonymous or pseudonymous customers, customers using VPNs or Tor, customers whose only verifiable identity is a self-custody wallet.

**Geographic risk** — crypto is borderless, so geographic risk is about where the customer is, where they transact, and where the VASP is licensed. A customer in a sanctioned jurisdiction using a VPN to appear elsewhere is a red flag.

**Transaction risk** — unusual patterns: large deposits from high-risk sources, rapid movement through mixers, interactions with darknet markets or ransomware addresses.

### Verification methods

**Identity verification** — government-issued ID, biometric verification (selfie matching), database verification (credit bureau, utility records). Same as traditional KYC but often more digital-native.

**Address verification** — proof of address via utility bill, bank statement or government correspondence. For crypto-native customers who may not have traditional banking, alternative methods include verified social media, professional profiles or notarized declarations.

**Source of funds** — for high-risk customers or large transactions, document the origin of crypto holdings: exchange purchase records, mining activity, trading history, inheritance, gifts. This is harder than traditional SoF because crypto lacks the paper trail of bank statements.

**Wallet screening** — screen the customer's declared wallet addresses against sanctions lists and chain-analytics databases before allowing deposits. An address with prior exposure to sanctioned entities or darknet markets is a red flag.

### Enhanced due diligence triggers

- Customer is a PEP or RCA
- Customer is in a high-risk jurisdiction
- Customer's wallet shows exposure to mixers, darknet markets or sanctioned entities
- Customer requests privacy features (e.g., Monero trading pairs)
- Customer's transaction patterns deviate significantly from stated purpose
- Customer is a business with unclear beneficial ownership

### Ongoing monitoring

Crypto customers require **continuous monitoring** rather than periodic reviews because transaction velocity is higher and risk can change rapidly. A customer who was low-risk at onboarding may become high-risk after their wallet receives funds from a ransomware address.

Monitoring should include:

- Real-time screening of deposit and withdrawal addresses
- Periodic re-screening of customer wallets against updated sanctions lists
- Behavioral analytics for unusual transaction patterns
- Trigger-based EDD refreshes (large transaction, jurisdiction change, adverse media)
