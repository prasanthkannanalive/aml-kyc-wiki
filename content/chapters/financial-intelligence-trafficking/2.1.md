# Translating Human Trafficking Risks into Financial Behavior

Human trafficking rarely appears in financial systems under its true name. Transactions are disguised as ordinary business activity, personal remittances, or service payments. Compliance professionals must therefore translate criminal behavior into financial signals. The FATF and Egmont Group (2020) identified that traffickers’ financial behavior often mirrors legitimate patterns — small businesses, family transfers, cross-border remittances — but with distinct irregularities once viewed over time. Key differences include:
- Purpose inconsistency: Transactions or account activity inconsistent with a customer’s profile or business type.
- Relationship anomalies: Multiple unrelated individuals funding or withdrawing from the same account.
- Behavioral irregularity: Abrupt fund movements through high-risk jurisdictions without business rationale. Understanding why traffickers use certain financial behaviors helps design targeted monitoring rules. Financial Behavior Model
1. Recruitment Phase: Payments to travel agents, employment firms, or training centers; advance deposits for “visa processing.”
2. Transport Phase: Clusters of remittances linked to known transit countries (e.g., Balkan corridor, East-to-Gulf routes).
3. Exploitation Phase: Regular wage deposits withdrawn immediately in cash or transferred to controllers.
4. Integration Phase: Funds reinvested in small retail, food, or entertainment businesses used as covers. In each phase, compliance analysts must map expected behavior to potential exploitation patterns — treating trafficking like a lifecycle rather than an isolated incident.
