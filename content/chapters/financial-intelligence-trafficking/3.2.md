Institutional Obligations Under AML/CFT Frameworks Compliance professionals play a legally mandated role in detecting and reporting financial activity linked to human trafficking. These responsibilities are embedded in institutional governance, operational procedures, and regulatory compliance reviews. 1. Enterprise-Level Risk Management Every regulated entity — whether a bank, fintech, or VASP — must integrate human trafficking into its Enterprise-Wide Risk Assessment (EWRA). Essential Steps: 1. Risk Identification: Determine which business lines (remittance, payroll, travel cards, digital wallets) are most vulnerable. 2. Risk Assessment: Evaluate customer profiles, geographies, and products with exposure to trafficking typologies. 3. Control Design: Embed trafficking flags into onboarding, TM, and STR workflows. 4. Ongoing Monitoring: Calibrate TM thresholds based on new typology trends or FIU feedback. Institutions accredited under the accreditor often adopt structured templates that link each FATF recommendation to corresponding control measures — ensuring traceability during compliance audits. 2. Compliance Governance and MLRO Accountability The Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) holds primary responsibility for ensuring the organization complies with trafficking-related obligations. Core MLRO Duties: • Approve and file STRs/SARs with FIUs. • Oversee high-risk customer reviews. • Maintain internal watchlists of entities flagged for trafficking risk. • Report to the Board and regulators on trafficking-related compliance exposure.
