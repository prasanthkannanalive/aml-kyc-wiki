Sanctions, Trade Compliance, and Financial Crime Convergence Human trafficking is increasingly connected to sanctioned entities, forced-labor supply chains, and transnational criminal networks. Understanding these linkages allows compliance professionals to build multi-layered risk detection systems. 1. Forced Labor and Supply Chain Sanctions A. U.S. OFAC and UFLPA The U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) enforces sanctions against companies using forced labor, particularly under the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA). Financial institutions must: • Screen suppliers, importers, and trade clients for human-rights sanctions exposure. • Flag entities in high-risk industries (textiles, agriculture, mining, construction). B. EU and UK Human Rights Regimes • The EU Global Human Rights Sanctions Regime (2020) empowers the EU to freeze assets linked to forced-labor networks. • The UK Modern Slavery Act (2015) requires companies to publish transparency statements — critical for financial institutions conducting KYC or EDD on corporate clients. 2. AML, Sanctions, and Trade Finance Intersections Financial trafficking networks exploit trade and supply chains by: • Misdeclaring goods or over/under-invoicing to move funds. • Using third-party logistics or shell import/export firms to launder proceeds. • Masking exploitation under legitimate trade finance transactions.
