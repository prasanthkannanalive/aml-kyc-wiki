Case Studies: How Financial Patterns Revealed Human Trafficking Case studies are the most practical way to understand how human trafficking activity manifests in financial data. Below are three examples adapted from FATF, FinCEN, and Egmont Group typology reports. Case 1: The “Recruitment Remittance Chain” Background: A mid-sized remittance provider in Europe noticed multiple small transfers (USD 100–250) being sent weekly from different customers to the same individual in Southeast Asia. The customers were not relatives and declared inconsistent purposes (“study fee,” “training payment,” “family support”). Financial Red Flags: • Shared receiver and overlapping sender profiles. • Transfers originating from employment agencies and cleaning companies. • Repeated usage of same IP address across multiple customer accounts. Outcome: Enhanced due diligence revealed that the recipient was running a fake overseas recruitment agency, charging migrant workers “placement fees.” These payments funded transport and exploitation operations in another country. The FIU linked the funds to a trafficking syndicate operating across three jurisdictions. Key Learning: Clusters of low-value remittances to a single beneficiary often indicate pooled victim payments or “collection wallets.” Linking sender behavior (employment, shared addresses, or identical transaction notes) reveals organized trafficking funding models. Case 2: Crypto Payments for Exploitative Advertising Background: A digital-asset exchange flagged a customer for converting large amounts of Bitcoin into smaller transactions directed to multiple wallets associated with social media advertising platforms.
