# Financial Intelligence Against Human Trafficking — Orientation

This course is built from the FIHT Official e-Study Book (v2.1) — certification preparation material for analysts who will encounter trafficking proceeds inside ordinary financial data. Human trafficking is not only a human-rights crime; it is a profit-driven enterprise, and every stage of it — recruitment, transport, exploitation, and reinvestment — leaves transactional traces that compliance professionals are trained to find.

## Why this discipline exists

The FATF treats human trafficking as a predicate offence to money laundering. That means the funds generated by exploitation are criminal proceeds, and the institutions that move them carry the same reporting obligations as for any other serious crime. Yet trafficking cases are chronically under-detected: the transactions are small, the victims are often the account holders, and the criminal infrastructure is deliberately fragmented across banks, fintechs, money-service businesses and virtual-asset platforms.

This course teaches the financial-intelligence method: how to read victim and trafficker behaviour in account data, which typologies recur across jurisdictions, and how to build a case file that a Financial Intelligence Unit can actually act on.

## How the course is organised

- **Foundations** — what trafficking is in financial terms, its scale and economics, and the legal framework that makes it reportable.
- **Detection** — red flags at account, transaction and network level; behavioural indicators that distinguish exploitation from legitimate remittances.
- **Analysis** — following the money: layering through fronts, money mules, informal value transfer and crypto off-ramps.
- **Reporting and cooperation** — writing STRs/SARs that survive FIU scrutiny, victim-safeguarding obligations, and cross-border information sharing.

## What makes this different from generic AML training

Generic AML training teaches you to spot structuring and sanctions exposure. This course adds the victim dimension: the same transaction pattern can be a crime scene or a coercion scene, and the correct response differs. An account receiving small daily deposits may be a wage-collection front controlling several victims — or it may be a legitimate domestic worker sending money home. The analyst's job is to gather enough evidence to tell the difference, and to escalate in a way that protects the people involved rather than endangering them.

## Before you start

You do not need prior trafficking expertise. You do need the standard AML toolkit — customer due diligence, transaction monitoring and suspicious-activity reporting — because this course applies all of it to one of the hardest predicate offences to prove. Work the chapters in order; each builds on the last.
