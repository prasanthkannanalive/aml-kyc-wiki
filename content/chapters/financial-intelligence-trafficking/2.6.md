Collaboration, Awareness, and Industry Initiatives Financial intelligence only reaches its potential when shared across public–private networks. The fight against human trafficking is now a multi-sectoral mission — connecting banks, fintechs, regulators, NGOs, and international bodies. 1. Public–Private Partnerships (PPPs) Governments and FIUs increasingly establish PPPs for information exchange. Notable examples include: • UK’s JMLIT (Joint Money Laundering Intelligence Taskforce) – pioneered cross-sector collaboration on trafficking cases. • Singapore AML/CFT Industry Partnership (ACIP) – focuses on cross-border labor exploitation typologies. • FATF Project Protect (Canada) – joint initiative among banks, law enforcement, and NGOs to identify trafficking financial flows. Benefits of PPPs: • Real-time typology updates. • Coordinated STR submissions by multiple banks. • Faster law enforcement response times. 2. NGO and Survivor-Led Collaboration Partnerships with NGOs provide non-financial intelligence—details of recruitment routes, victim testimonies, and cash-flow methods. Institutions can incorporate this data into: • TM model calibration. • Sectoral risk assessments. • Staff training and awareness sessions. Examples: Thorn, Stop the Traffik, and Polaris Project have each partnered with FIUs to translate field data into financial indicators. 3. Industry and Technology Collaboration RegTech and blockchain analytics firms now play a critical role. Platforms like TRM Labs or Elliptic offer blockchain typology libraries that identify wallets associated with trafficking rings. Meanwhile, SWIFT’s Financial Crime Compliance Programme promotes shared analytics among correspondent banks to detect illicit corridors. 4.
