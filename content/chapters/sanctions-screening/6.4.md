Escalation Escalation is where an alert goes when it cannot be decided. It is a correct outcome, not a failure to reach one, and the difference between a good escalation and a poor one is almost entirely in what is written. 6.4.1  When to escalate Five situations, and an analyst should recognise them without deliberation. The identifiers are absent rather than different, and cannot be obtained. This is the commonest by a wide margin. The identifiers point both ways — one matches and another differs. A matching date of birth with a differing passport number is not a discount and not a confirmation; it is a contradiction requiring more senior judgement. The question is legal rather than factual. Control under the EU or UK test, potential circumvention, a conflict of laws, whether a general licence applies. Module 4 and Module 2 both closed on this point. The match appears genuine. A probable true match escalates immediately and is not worked further at analyst level, because the next steps involve freezing, reporting and notification. Anything unusual about the request itself. A customer who declines to answer an identity question, a correspondent who provides evasive information, a relationship manager applying pressure to release. These are not identity findings and they are material. 6.4.2  What escalation is not It is not a way of moving a difficult alert off your queue. An escalation without analysis transfers the work rather than advancing it, and a reviewer receiving an alert with no attempt at identifiers has to start from the beginning. It is not an admission that you could not do your job. The four-question method has two outcomes that are not decisions, and reaching one of them correctly is doing the job. It is not a substitute for requesting information. Where the missing data can be obtained, obtaining it is the next step; escalating instead skips the cheapest available resolution. And it is not permanent. An escalated alert returns with a decision, and the analyst who escalated should see what happened. An escalation process that does not close the loop teaches nobody anything and produces the same escalation next month. 6.4.3  What an escalation record contains Four elements, and the third is the one usually missing. The match: which list, which entry, what score, which field, primary name or alias. What was established: every identifier compared, with values and sources on both sides, including those that matched. 105 What could not be established, and what was tried. 'Date of birth not held on the payment; request for information issued to the correspondent on 12 August, no response as at 14 August' is a complete statement. 'Insufficient information' is not. The specific question being escalated. 'Please advise' is not a question. 'The listed entry carries a date of birth range 1971–1974; the customer record shows 1973, which falls inside it. No other identifier is available on either side. Is the residual risk acceptable, or should the relationship be reviewed?' is one. WHAT TO TAKE FROM THIS CHAPTER — Escalate when identifiers are absent and cannot be obtained; when they point both ways; when the question is legal; when the match appears genuine; and when anything about the request itself is unusual. — A probable true match escalates immediately and is not worked further at analyst level. — Escalation without analysis transfers work rather than advancing it. — Where information can be obtained, obtaining it comes first. Escalating instead skips the cheapest resolution. — An escalation that does not come back closed teaches nobody anything and recurs. — The record needs the match, what was established, what was not and what was tried, and a specific question. — 'Insufficient information' and 'please advise' are both non-statements. 106
