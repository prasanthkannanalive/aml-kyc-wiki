# Travel bans, transport and aviation measures

Not every sanction is financial, and the non-financial measures matter to a bank more than is generally recognised — partly because they generate alerts on assets rather than people, and partly because a travel ban tells you something about a customer relationship.


**3.5.1** Travel bans A travel ban obliges states to prevent the entry into or transit through their territory of designated individuals. It is enforced by immigration authorities rather than by financial institutions, and no bank is expected to police it. It nonetheless matters to a compliance function for two reasons. First, travel bans and asset freezes are usually imposed together, so a party subject to one is very often subject to the other; if your data shows only a travel ban, that is a prompt to check the financial measures rather than a conclusion that none apply. Second, the existence of a travel ban is a material fact about a customer relationship — a private banking client who cannot enter the jurisdiction in which their relationship manager sits is a relationship that requires explanation. Exemptions exist for humanitarian need, judicial process and the fulfilment of religious obligations, and a state may permit entry where its own courts require it.


**3.5.2** Vessel designations and port measures Vessels are designated in their own right, identified by IMO number and by name. Module 7 covers vessel screening in full; the point here is that a vessel designation is a distinct measure with distinct effects. Designating a vessel typically prohibits port entry, the provision of bunkering and other services, and in some regimes the provision of insurance and classification. The vessel becomes commercially unusable in compliant markets even where its owner is not designated. Port entry bans operate at jurisdiction level as well. The EU and UK have prohibited access to their ports by Russian-flagged vessels, subject to exceptions for food, humanitarian goods and certain energy products. These are territorial measures rather than designations and do not appear on any list of names. For a bank, vessel measures surface through trade finance and marine insurance rather than through payments. A letter of credit naming a designated vessel as the carrier is a problem even where every party is clean.


**3.5.3** Aircraft measures Aviation measures take several forms and expanded substantially after 2022. Aircraft are designated individually by registration and serial number, in the same way vessels are, and appear on the SDN List and its equivalents. Overflight and landing bans operate territorially: the airspace of the imposing jurisdiction is closed to aircraft registered in, operated by or owned or controlled by persons connected to the target state. Export restrictions on aircraft, parts and aviation technology have proved among the more effective measures in practice, because commercial aviation depends on a continuous supply of 55 certified parts and on manufacturer maintenance support. An airline denied both faces a progressive loss of airworthy fleet that no amount of domestic substitution readily solves. Leasing restrictions matter for financiers. Prohibitions on leasing aircraft to designated parties, and requirements to terminate existing leases, created a substantial dispute over aircraft stranded in Russia after 2022 and a long-running set of insurance claims that is still working through the courts.


## What to take from this chapter
- Travel bans are enforced by immigration authorities, not banks, but they usually accompany an asset freeze — treat one as a prompt to check the other
- A travel ban is a material fact about a customer relationship even though it imposes no obligation on the bank
- Vessels are designated in their own right by IMO number and name, which makes them commercially unusable even where the owner is clean
- Port entry bans are territorial measures and appear on no list of names
- Vessel measures reach banks through trade finance and marine insurance rather than through payments
- Aircraft parts and maintenance restrictions have been unusually effective because certified supply cannot readily be substituted
- Leasing prohibitions and termination requirements created the stranded-aircraft disputes still in litigation.
