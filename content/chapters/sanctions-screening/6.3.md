Discounting an entity Entities have different identifiers from individuals and fail in different ways. This chapter covers what distinguishes two companies, what does not, and the question entity discounting can never answer on its own. 6.3.1  Strong entity identifiers A differing company registration number in the same jurisdiction is the strongest available factor. Registration numbers are unique within their register, which makes a mismatch close to conclusive — provided both sides genuinely refer to a registration and not to a tax number, a licence number or a branch code, which are frequently confused in payment data. A differing jurisdiction of incorporation, where both are established from registry evidence rather than from an address. A company incorporated in Singapore is not a company incorporated in the British Virgin Islands, whatever similarity the names carry. The qualification is that groups operate entities with near-identical names in multiple jurisdictions by design, so a jurisdiction difference distinguishes the legal person and does not by itself distinguish the group. A differing legal form, where reliably evidenced — a GmbH is not an AG, a public limited company is not a private one. This is weaker than it looks because payment data drops and abbreviates legal form suffixes constantly, and because groups hold entities of several forms under one name. A registered address matching the register rather than the list, again with the caveat from Module 5 that addresses are weak for discounting. 6.3.2  What does not distinguish two entities A different sector or line of business. Sanctions records describe entities in terms drawn from the designating authority's reasoning, not from a companies register, and a trading company designated for procurement activity may be described in ways that do not match its own commercial self-description at all. Front companies exist precisely to present an ordinary line of business. A different trading name. Entities trade under names other than their registered ones, and sanctions records frequently carry both. A mismatch against one may be a match against another on the same entry. The absence of a group relationship. Establishing that your counterparty is not part of the designated entity's group answers a question about corporate structure, not about whether it is the designated entity — and it also does not answer the ownership question below. Longevity or apparent substance. A company with a real office, real employees and a twenty-year history can be designated. Substance is not evidence of a different identity. The same considerations from the individual chapter apply: a different spelling is why the alert fired, prior transactions are not evidence, and nothing found is not a discount. 6.3.3  Corporate name variation Entity names vary in ways that generate alerts and in ways that genuinely distinguish, and telling them apart is a skill worth building. 102 Variations that mean nothing: legal form present, absent or abbreviated; the ampersand against the word and; punctuation and spacing; the word limited against ltd; transliteration of a name originally in another script; and the presence or absence of filler words such as group, holding, international and trading, which appear in enormous numbers of unrelated entities. Variations that may mean something: a different distinctive element — the part of the name that is not generic. Alpha Trading International and Alpha Shipping International share two generic tokens and differ in the one that identifies. That is a real difference and worth pursuing, though not conclusive on its own. The practical technique is to identify the distinctive element on both sides and compare those, discounting the generic tokens entirely. A match on holding, international and trading is a match on nothing. This is the entity equivalent of the nisba problem in Module 5. 6.3.4  The question entity discounting cannot answer You may establish conclusively that your counterparty is not the designated entity. That does not establish that your counterparty is not blocked. Module 4 explained why: an entity owned fifty per cent or more by designated persons is blocked without appearing on any list. A perfect discount on identity leaves the ownership question entirely open, and the two are separate analyses on separate data. This is not a reason to escalate every entity alert. It is a reason to be precise about what the disposition claims. 'Discounted — different registration number, confirmed against the Companies House register' is an accurate statement about identity. It is not a statement that the entity is clear, and a rationale that reads as though it were overstates the analysis. Where the alerting entity sits in a jurisdiction or sector associated with the designated party's network, or where the designation is recent and the network is being mapped, the ownership question is worth asking on its own account — and the answer belongs in a different part of the file. The analyst obtains the beneficiary's registration certificate: incorporated in Singapore, registration number 201814927K, no connection to the designated entity, which is registered in Cyprus with a different number. The rationale reads: 'Confirmed different entity — Singapore registration 201814927K against Cyprus registration of the listed party. Entity is clear.' Question.  What is wrong with an otherwise good piece of work? Answer.  The analysis is correct and the last three words are not. Two different registrations in two jurisdictions is a strong identity discount and the sourcing is exactly right. But 'entity is clear' claims more than was established: the analysis addressed whether the beneficiary is the designated entity, and did not address whether the beneficiary is owned or controlled by designated persons — which would block it without any listing. The accurate closing sentence is 'confirmed not the listed entity; ownership not assessed'. That is a smaller claim and a defensible one. This matters beyond wording, because the next analyst to see this counterparty will read 'entity is clear' as covering ground it never covered, and the file will carry that error forward.
