Why vessels are screened A vessel is property, not a person, and yet it is designated in its own right and carries prohibitions that attach to it wherever it goes. This chapter explains why, and where a bank's exposure to it actually arises. 7.1.1  What a vessel designation does Vessels appear on the SDN List and its equivalents as blocked property. The designation attaches to the ship itself, identified by IMO number and name, and it operates independently of who owns her. The prohibitions that follow are practical rather than financial. Port entry is refused in jurisdictions applying the measure. Bunkering, towage, pilotage, crewing, provisioning, repair and classification services may not be supplied. Insurance and reinsurance may not be provided. Cargo may not lawfully be carried for parties subject to the measures. The commercial effect is that a designated vessel becomes unusable in compliant markets even where every party dealing with her is clean. She cannot refuel in most ports, cannot be insured by a mainstream club, and cannot discharge at a terminal that screens. Note the direction of the extension. A designated owner blocks the vessel, because the vessel is the owner's property. But a designated vessel does not block her owner, her charterer or her cargo — those are separate parties requiring separate analysis. Confusing the two produces the same category of error as blocking a parent because its subsidiary is designated. 7.1.2  Where a bank's exposure arises Very little maritime exposure reaches a bank through a payment message. Payment messages name payers and payees; they do not usually name ships. The exposure arrives instead through trade finance. A letter of credit names the vessel, the ports of loading and discharge, and the carrier. A bill of lading names the vessel and evidences the voyage. A charter party names the vessel and the parties to the hire. Documentary collections carry the same information. It also arrives through marine insurance, where the institution is an insurer or a broker rather than a bank, and through vessel finance, where the ship is the security for a loan. 112 The practical consequence is that vessel screening is a trade and documentary control, not a payments control. An institution whose only screening is on payment messages has no exposure to this material — and no visibility of it either, which is a different problem. 7.1.3  Who else is screening Understanding the wider control environment helps an analyst calibrate what their own institution is contributing. Flag registries screen at registration, with widely varying rigour. Classification societies screen before issuing or maintaining class. Protection and indemnity clubs in the International Group screen before writing cover, and their withdrawal of cover is often the effective sanction. Port authorities and terminals screen on arrival. Bunker suppliers screen before delivering fuel. The evasion architecture covered in Chapter 7.4 exists precisely to route around all of them: registries that do not screen, insurance outside the International Group, classification by societies with no Western exposure, and ports that do not check. For a bank this means that a vessel appearing in your documents has usually passed several checks already, and the ones that matter are whether those checks were performed by parties who would refuse. WHAT TO TAKE FROM THIS CHAPTER — A vessel designation attaches to the ship, identified by IMO number, and operates independently of ownership. — It prohibits services — port entry, bunkering, insurance, classification, crewing — rather than financial dealings alone. — A designated owner blocks the vessel. A designated vessel does not block her owner, charterer or cargo. — Bank exposure arrives through trade finance documents, marine insurance and ship finance, not through payment messages. — An institution screening only payments has no visibility of maritime exposure at all. — Registries, class societies, P&I clubs, ports and bunker suppliers all screen. Evasion architecture exists to route around every one of them. 113
