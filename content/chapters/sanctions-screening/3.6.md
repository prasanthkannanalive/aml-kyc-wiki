# Correspondent banking and financial messaging

The final category restricts the plumbing rather than the parties: access to correspondent accounts, to clearing systems and to financial messaging. These measures are among the most consequential imposed and among the least visible in a payment message.


**3.6.1** Correspondent account restrictions A correspondent account is what allows a bank without a presence in a currency's home jurisdiction to make and receive payments in it. Restricting access to correspondent accounts therefore restricts access to the currency itself. Two mechanisms produce this. The US CAPTA List identifies foreign financial institutions subject to prohibitions or strict conditions on maintaining a correspondent or payable-through account in the United States. And FinCEN's Section 311 special measures, discussed in Module 2, can prohibit US institutions from maintaining correspondent accounts for a designated foreign bank, jurisdiction or account class. Neither blocks assets. Both are, in commercial terms, more severe than many blocking designations, because an internationally active bank without dollar clearing cannot serve its customers. The screening consequence is that these restrictions concern relationships rather than transactions. They are managed by correspondent banking and financial institution relationship teams, and an alert on a payment is usually the wrong place to catch one. An institution whose only control is payment screening has no line of sight to a restriction of this kind.


**3.6.2** Financial messaging Restricting access to financial messaging is a distinct measure with a specific history. In 2012 and again in 2022, EU regulations required SWIFT to disconnect designated banks from its messaging network. The effect is often overstated and worth stating precisely. SWIFT is a messaging service, not a payment or settlement system; it carries instructions between institutions. Disconnection does not freeze assets or prohibit payments as a matter of law — it removes the standard channel through which instructions travel. The practical effect is nonetheless severe, because the alternatives — bilateral messaging, telex, correspondent workarounds, national messaging systems — are slower, less automated, and available to far fewer counterparties. Volume collapses even where no transaction is technically prohibited. For an analyst the point to hold is that disconnection and designation are different things. A bank may be disconnected without being designated, in which case dealing with it is not prohibited but is operationally difficult; or designated without being disconnected, in which case the messaging works and the dealing is unlawful. Reading one as the other produces both errors.


**3.6.3** Central bank measures Measures against a central bank are the largest financial sanctions imposed and deserve mention because they behave unlike anything else. The 2022 restrictions on the Central Bank of Russia prohibited transactions related to the management of its reserves and assets, immobilising a substantial portion of the reserves held in Western jurisdictions. Comparable measures have been applied to the central banks of Iran, Venezuela, Afghanistan and Syria. The distinctive feature is that the restriction is imposed on transactions relating to reserve management rather than by blocking a named account, because central bank reserves are held in many forms across many institutions. Identifying what is caught requires knowing what the assets are and whose reserves they represent, not matching a name on a payment. The immobilised assets have become a live policy question — whether they may be confiscated, whether the returns they generate may be used, and under what legal theory. That question is unresolved and is not one an analyst needs to answer, but it is worth knowing that immobilisation and confiscation are legally distinct and that the current position is the former.


## Scenario — THE BANK THAT WAS DISCONNECTED BUT NOT DESIGNATED

A corporate customer instructs a payment to a supplier whose account is held at a foreign bank. The bank has been disconnected from SWIFT under an EU measure. It is not designated by the EU, the UK or the US. The customer proposes routing the payment through a second institution that maintains a bilateral arrangement with the disconnected bank. **Question.** Is the proposed routing a sanctions problem? **Answer.** Not necessarily, and the honest answer requires separating three questions. Disconnection is not designation: dealing with a disconnected bank is not prohibited by that measure alone, so a payment reaching it through an alternative channel is not automatically unlawful. But two further questions arise immediately.

First, whether the disconnected bank is designated under any regime the institution is subject to, including one it has not checked — disconnection usually accompanies other measures. Second, and more important, whether the proposed routing is structured to circumvent a restriction, because circumvention is itself prohibited under EU, UK and US regimes even where the underlying transaction would otherwise be permitted. A customer proposing an unusual route specifically because the normal one is closed is describing the shape of circumvention, and that is what the escalation should say.


## What to take from this chapter
- Correspondent account restrictions remove access to a currency and are often more severe commercially than a blocking designation
- CAPTA and Section 311 measures concern relationships, not transactions. Payment screening alone will not see them
- SWIFT disconnection removes a messaging channel. It does not freeze assets or prohibit payments as a matter of law
- A bank may be disconnected without being designated, or designated without being disconnected. Reading one as the other produces both errors
- Central bank measures restrict transactions relating to reserve management rather than blocking a named account
- Immobilisation and confiscation are legally distinct; the current position on immobilised reserves is the former
- Circumvention is separately prohibited.
