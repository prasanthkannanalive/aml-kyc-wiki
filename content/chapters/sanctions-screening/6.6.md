# When the match is real

Most of your alerts are false positives. Occasionally one is not, and the hour after that becomes clear has its own sequence. This chapter covers confirmation, the choice between freezing and rejecting, reporting, and the one thing you must not do.


**6.6.1** Confirm against the source Before anything is frozen, confirm the entry against the publishing authority's own record rather than against the provider extract that produced the alert. Three things can be wrong in the extract: the entry may have been amended or delisted since the last load; the provider's normalisation may have altered a field; and the programme tag determining what is prohibited may not have carried through. Module 5 covered why. This takes minutes and it establishes the basis for everything that follows. A freeze imposed on a delisted entry is an unlawful interference with the customer's property, and 'our system said so' is not an answer. Record the confirmation: which authority's list, retrieved when, entry reference. This becomes the foundation of the report.


**6.6.2** Freeze, block, reject Three different actions with different legal meanings, and Module 3 set out why confusing them causes breaches. Block or freeze where the property has come within your possession or control. The funds are placed in a blocked account, immobilised, and held. They are not returned to the sender, which would be a prohibited dealing and would put funds back into the hands of a party who may themselves be restricted. Reject where the property has not come into your possession — an instruction you decline to execute, an application you refuse. Nothing is held because nothing was received. The distinction is factual and turns on where the funds are, not on which outcome is preferable. Where it is unclear, that is itself an escalation: it determines both the legal characterisation and the reporting obligation. Whichever applies, the action is taken before the customer is contacted and before any explanation is given.


**6.6.3** Report Reporting obligations are specific, time-limited and independently enforceable, and the timescales run from the action rather than from the investigation concluding. In the United States, blocked property is reported to OFAC within ten business days of blocking, and rejected transactions within ten business days of rejection. An annual report of blocked property held as at 30 June is due by 30 September. In the United Kingdom, a relevant firm must inform OFSI as soon as practicable and must report frozen assets held. Failure to report is a criminal offence in its own right, separate from any dealing. In the EU, obligations sit in each regulation and run to the national competent authority of the member state concerned. Internal reporting matters as much. The MLRO or equivalent, the relevant business line, and where the party is a customer rather than a counterparty, the relationship owner. A frozen customer account is a relationship event as well as a compliance one, and discovering it from the customer is worse for everybody.


**6.6.4** Do not tip off The one prohibition in this chapter that is easy to breach without meaning to. Telling a customer that a payment has been blocked because of a sanctions match, before the institution has determined what may be disclosed, risks alerting the designated party. It gives them the opportunity to move other assets, route future business elsewhere, or destroy evidence. The obligations differ between AML tipping-off, which is a specific statutory offence in many jurisdictions, and sanctions disclosure, which is governed by the applicable regime and by legal advice. What holds across both is that the analyst does not decide what to tell the customer. The practical form this takes: a customer asks why their payment has not arrived, and the answer is that it is under review and the relevant team will make contact. Not that it has been blocked, not which list, not which party. Relationship managers frequently want more to say, and the pressure to explain is real. It is not a decision at their level either, and an escalation is the correct response to being pressed.


## Scenario — THE HOUR AFTER

You confirm a true match on an inbound payment of $1.1m for a corporate customer. The ordering party is an SDN. The funds have been credited to your suspense account. Within twenty minutes the relationship manager calls: the customer is on the phone, the payment is for a shipment already loaded, and the customer wants to know what has happened so they can instruct the sender to resend from another account. **Question.** What do you do, and what do you say? **Answer.** The funds are in your possession, so this is a block rather than a rejection: they are moved to a blocked account, immobilised, and reported to OFAC within ten business days.

They are not returned to the sender under any circumstances, and the customer's proposed remedy — resending from another account — describes circumvention and must be recorded rather than facilitated. On what to say: not that a sanctions match has been identified, not which party, not which list. The relationship manager is told that the payment is under review, that the customer should be told the same, and that any further communication will be issued by compliance after the position has been settled. The pressure will be considerable and the payment being for a loaded shipment does not change any of it. Escalate immediately, including the customer's suggestion, which is now a material fact in the file.


## What to take from this chapter
- Confirm against the publishing authority's own record before freezing. Extracts go stale and normalisation alters fields
- Block where the funds are in your possession; reject where they never arrived. The distinction is factual, not preferential
- Never return blocked funds to the sender — it is a prohibited dealing and puts money back with a possibly restricted party
- US: report blocked property and rejected transactions within ten business days, plus the annual 30 June return
- UK: report as soon as practicable; failure to report is a separate criminal offence
- Internal notification matters too. Discovering a frozen account from the customer is worse for everyone
- Do not tell the customer a payment was blocked for a sanctions match. That is not an analyst-level disclosure decision
- A customer proposing to resend from another account is describing circumvention.
