Comprehensive and territorial measures A small number of regimes still restrict a jurisdiction rather than a list of parties. They are the exception now, but they carry the widest prohibitions you will meet and the largest general licence structures, and the analysis they require is different in kind from a name match. 3.2.1  What survives of the comprehensive model Comprehensive country programmes have not disappeared. In the US framework, Cuba, Iran, North Korea and Syria have historically been the comprehensive programmes, alongside the Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk regions of Ukraine which are treated territorially. The distinguishing feature is that the prohibition attaches to the jurisdiction rather than to a party. Under a comprehensive programme, US persons are generally prohibited from dealing with the country, its government, and persons located there, regardless of whether any specific individual is designated. Exports, imports, investment, services and financial dealings are all caught unless authorised. This changes the screening question. Under a targeted regime you ask whether any party is designated. Under a comprehensive regime you ask where the parties are, where the goods are going, where the services will be delivered, and whether any element of the transaction touches the jurisdiction. Location fields, addresses, routing, ports of discharge and country of origin become as important as names. It also changes what a clean name search means. A counterparty in Tehran with no designation whatever is still a prohibited counterparty for a US person, and a screening system that returns no hit has answered a question you did not need to ask. 3.2.2  Territorial measures and disputed regions The Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk measures are worth separate treatment because they raise a problem that country-level restrictions do not. A territorial measure applies to a region rather than a recognised state. That means the address data your systems hold may not identify it. A company registered in Simferopol may show a Ukrainian address, or a Russian one, depending on when and by whom the record was created. Postal codes, oblast names and city spellings all vary. There is no country field value that captures it. The same problem appears with goods. Determining whether a commodity originated in a restricted region requires documentation about origin, not about the seller's registered address — and origin documentation is exactly what an evader falsifies. The practical control is not name screening. It is address screening against region-specific place names, combined with trade documentation review and, for commodities, questions about the actual point of loading. An institution relying on the country field alone will not see Crimea at all. 3.2.3  General licences as the operating mechanism A comprehensive prohibition that admitted no exceptions would be unworkable, so comprehensive programmes carry extensive general licence structures, and in practice a great deal of lawful activity with these jurisdictions happens under them. 46 A general licence authorises a described category of activity without any application being made. If your transaction falls within its terms, it is authorised — you do not apply, and you do not receive a document. The licence is the authority. Typical categories include personal remittances, informational materials, telecommunications, internet-based communications services, agricultural commodities, medicine and medical devices, and activities of international organisations and NGOs. Two disciplines follow. First, general licences have conditions, and the conditions are the licence: an agricultural exemption that excludes certain fertilisers, or a remittance authorisation limited to non-commercial family transfers, authorises only what it says. Second, they change. General licences are amended, superseded and allowed to expire, and an institution operating on the terms of a licence that was replaced eighteen months ago is operating without authority. When a transaction proceeds under a general licence, the disposition must identify it by number. 'Released under a humanitarian exemption' is not a record. 'Released under General Licence 8, agricultural commodities, conditions at paragraph (b) met' is. 3.2.4  Specific licences and when they are needed Where no general licence covers the activity, the route is a specific licence: an application to the competent authority for authorisation of a described transaction. The process is slow. Timescales of several months are normal in most jurisdictions, and complex applications take longer. Applications are decided against a stated licensing policy, which for many programmes is a presumption of denial — meaning the applicant must displace an assumption that the licence will not be granted. An institution should know three things about specific licences. Who applies: usually the party whose activity is restricted, not the bank, though banks apply for authority to release blocked funds. What the licence covers: precisely the transaction described, not similar transactions later. And what it does not do: a licence from OFAC does not authorise anything under EU or UK law, and a transaction touching several regimes needs authority from each. For an analyst, the point at which a specific licence becomes relevant is a point of escalation, not of decision. Identifying that no general licence applies and that authorisation would be needed is the analyst's contribution; deciding to apply is not. 47 SCENARIO — THE MEDICINE SHIPMENT THAT HAD NO ADDRESS PROBLEM A pharmaceutical wholesaler instructs payment for a shipment of antibiotics to a distributor. The distributor's registered address is in Dubai and screening returns no hit on any list. The shipping documents show a port of discharge in a country under comprehensive measures. The analyst, seeing a clean name screen and a UAE address, is preparing to release. Question.  What has been missed? Answer.  The question that the regime actually asks. Under a comprehensive programme the prohibition attaches to the jurisdiction, so a clean name screen answers nothing — the relevant facts are the port of discharge and the ultimate destination, both of which are on the documents in front of the analyst. The correct analysis is whether the transaction is prohibited by reason of destination and, if so, whether it falls within a general licence for medicine and medical devices. It very likely does, since most comprehensive programmes authorise medicine. But the release must then be recorded under that licence by number, with the conditions checked — some medical general licences exclude specified items or require the payment route to avoid designated banks. Releasing on the basis that the name screen was clean would reach the right outcome for entirely the wrong reason, and would be indefensible if the shipment had been something else.
