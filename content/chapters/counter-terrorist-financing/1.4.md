# Why AML controls miss it

This chapter closes the module by naming, precisely, the ways in which a well-run AML function fails against terrorist financing. Not because it is badly built, but because it was built for a different problem.


**1.4.1** What AML monitoring is designed to find A conventional transaction monitoring system encodes a set of assumptions about criminal money. It is worth stating them explicitly, because each one is false in a TF context. That criminal funds enter the system from an unexplained source. TF funds frequently enter from a payslip. That criminal funds must be moved, converted or layered to be usable. TF funds are usable as they are. That the value involved is material. TF value is trivial. That deviation from an established pattern is the signal. TF activity is frequently indistinguishable from the customer's established pattern, because the customer is doing what they have always done and one of the payments has a different purpose. Every one of those assumptions is sound for laundering. Together they describe a system that will pass terrorist financing without registering it, while functioning exactly as specified.


**1.4.2** Where the two disciplines do overlap It would be wrong to conclude that AML controls contribute nothing, and an analyst should know where they genuinely help. Where an organisation funds itself through crime — narcotics, fraud, extortion at scale — the proceeds require laundering and behave like laundered money. AML monitoring works on that portion. Customer due diligence is common infrastructure. Knowing who a customer is, what they do and where their money comes from serves both purposes, and a weak CDD file is a weak CDD file regardless of which risk you are assessing. Sanctions screening is shared, and is the single most productive CFT control an institution operates — because designated terrorist entities appear on the lists that screening already covers. Module 5 covers this in full. And the reporting infrastructure is the same: the same financial intelligence unit, frequently the same report form, the same internal escalation path. So the correct statement is not that AML is useless against TF. It is that AML addresses one part of the TF problem — the criminally-funded part — and is structurally blind to the rest.


**1.4.3** What has to be different Four things, and they preview the rest of this guide. Identity does the work that value does in AML. Since the transaction is uninformative, screening the parties against terrorism designations is the primary control rather than a supplementary one. Pattern replaces threshold. What matters is a sequence over time — regular small transfers to a jurisdiction of concern, cash extraction followed by conversion to stored value, credit taken with no repayment — rather than any single amount. Context is decisive and mostly external. Financial institutions do not hold the information that turns an indicator into a case. That is why the reporting relationship with the financial intelligence unit matters more here than anywhere else in compliance: you are supplying a fragment to somebody who holds the rest. And the tolerance for false positives is different, in both directions. TF indicators are weak and generic, so acting on them alone produces a great many wrong answers about innocent people — disproportionately from particular communities and corridors. Module 4 treats that as a first-order problem rather than a footnote, because getting it wrong causes real harm and produces bad intelligence at the same time.


## Scenario — THE ALERT THAT NEVER FIRED

After an attack, a review of the perpetrator's banking finds: eight months of ordinary salaried activity; three personal loans from three institutions in six weeks totalling €9,000, none repaid; a €4,000 cash withdrawal; and a series of small transfers to two individuals abroad. No monitoring alert was generated at any point by any of the three banks. **Question.** Was that a control failure? **Answer.** Partly, and the distinction matters. The small transfers and the salaried activity would not have alerted anywhere, and no reasonable system would have made them. The €4,000 withdrawal is unremarkable in isolation.

But three unsecured loans from three institutions inside six weeks, none repaid, is one of the few genuinely detectable lawful-source patterns in terrorist financing — and it is invisible to each bank individually while being obvious across the three. That is not a tuning failure; it is a structural one, and it is why credit bureau data and information-sharing arrangements between institutions matter more in CFT than in most of compliance. The honest post-incident finding is that each institution's monitoring worked as designed, and the design could not see the case.


## What to take from this chapter
- AML monitoring assumes unexplained sources, layering, material value and deviation from pattern. All four fail in TF
- A system that passes a TF case may be functioning exactly as specified — this is a design limit, not a defect
- AML genuinely helps where the organisation funds itself through crime, since those proceeds behave like laundered money
- CDD, sanctions screening and the reporting infrastructure are shared, and screening is the most productive CFT control an institution has
- Identity replaces value, and pattern replaces threshold
- Context is decisive and mostly external — the institution supplies a fragment to somebody holding the rest
