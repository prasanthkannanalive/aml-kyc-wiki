# Why behaviour beats thresholds

Module 1 established that value carries almost no information in terrorist financing. This chapter sets out what carries information instead, and how to think about a signal that only exists across time.


**4.1.1** The single transaction tells you nothing It is worth stating this plainly at the start of the detection module, because everything else follows from it. A transfer of four hundred euros to a personal account abroad is, considered alone, entirely uninformative. It is the shape of a remittance, a gift, a shared expense, a repayment, a subscription — and, occasionally, terrorist financing. Nothing about the transaction distinguishes those cases. The same is true of a cash withdrawal, a prepaid card purchase, a small credit facility, or a donation to an online appeal. Each is ordinary in isolation and remains ordinary however carefully you examine it. So detection in this field is not the examination of transactions. It is the recognition of sequences — combinations across time, across accounts, and sometimes across institutions. That is a different analytical act from the one AML alert review trains, and it is why an analyst who works TF alerts the way they work AML alerts will find nothing and correctly conclude there was nothing to find.


**4.1.2** What a pattern actually is A pattern in this context has three properties, and all three must be present for it to mean anything. Multiple elements. One withdrawal is a withdrawal. A withdrawal, a prepaid card purchase and a vehicle hire within a fortnight is a sequence. A relationship between the elements that is not coincidental. The elements should fit together in a way that suggests a purpose — cash extracted, converted to an anonymous instrument, spent on something operational. Three unrelated ordinary events happening in the same month is not a pattern; it is a month. And a departure from what this customer normally does, or from what a customer like this would normally do. This is the weakest of the three and the most easily abused, because judgements about what a customer 'like this' does are exactly where bias enters. Chapter 4.4 returns to it. What a pattern is not: a list of individually unremarkable facts assembled until they feel like enough. That is the analytical failure this module exists to prevent, and it is the same failure the sanctions discipline calls the null discount — a conclusion built from an absence rather than from evidence.


**4.1.3** Time as the operative variable Terrorist financing patterns unfold over weeks or months, which has three practical consequences. Alert windows matter. A monitoring rule that looks at a rolling seven days will not see a pattern that develops over eleven weeks. Rules built for laundering, where velocity is often the signal, are frequently windowed too tightly for this. Case history matters. If an alert on a customer is closed and the file is not readable by whoever sees the next alert three weeks later, each alert is reviewed as if it were the first. The pattern only exists in the aggregate, so an analyst who cannot see the aggregate cannot see the case. And the end of the pattern is the point at which action becomes urgent. The sequence typically terminates in something operational — travel, a purchase, a transfer to a facilitator. An institution's chance of contributing usefully is highest early and falls to nothing after. This is the strongest practical argument for good rationale-writing in CFT. The next analyst's ability to see a pattern depends entirely on what the previous one wrote down.


## What to take from this chapter
- A single TF transaction is uninformative and remains so however carefully it is examined
- Detection is the recognition of sequences across time, accounts and sometimes institutions — a different act from AML alert review
- A pattern needs multiple elements, a non-coincidental relationship between them, and a genuine departure from normal
- A list of unremarkable facts assembled until it feels like enough is not a pattern; it is the same failure as a null discount
- Rules windowed for laundering velocity are frequently too tight to see a pattern developing over eleven weeks
- The pattern exists only in aggregate, so an analyst who cannot read the case history cannot see the case
- Rationale quality is a detection control in CFT, not just a record — the next analyst sees only what you wrote.
