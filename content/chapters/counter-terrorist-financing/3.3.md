Cash couriers and bulk cash A significant share of terrorist financing never touches a financial institution at all. This chapter covers physical cash movement — why it persists, how it is regulated, and what an institution can and cannot see. 3.3.1  Why cash persists Cash has properties nothing else matches, and the same properties that make it useful for terrorist financing make it universal for everything else. It leaves no transaction record. There is no message, no counterparty field, no screening opportunity. It is anonymous at the point of use. Possession is the whole of the claim. It works where nothing else does. In conflict zones, in areas without banking, in economies where cash is simply the normal medium. And it requires no infrastructure — no account, no identification, no institution, no permission. For a terrorist organisation, several income streams are cash by nature: extortion collected locally, ransom payments, proceeds from local trade or taxation in an area of control. Module 1 covered these. None of that money needs to enter the banking system to be useful, because it is spent in the same economy it was collected in. 3.3.2  Bulk cash smuggling Where cash must cross a border, it is carried — concealed in vehicles, in baggage, in freight, on the person, or divided among multiple couriers travelling separately. Recommendation 32 addresses this. It requires countries to operate a declaration or disclosure system for cross-border transport of currency and bearer instruments above a threshold, to have the power to stop or restrain undeclared or suspected amounts, to impose sanctions for false declaration, and to allow confiscation where the funds relate to terrorist financing or money laundering. The system is genuinely useful and its limits are obvious. It applies at borders, so it catches what crosses a declared frontier and misses what does not. Thresholds are typically set around ten thousand units of currency — which, as Module 1 established, is far above the cost of most attacks. And enforcement depends on customs resources that vary enormously. The pattern that most often defeats it is division: several couriers each carrying an amount below the declaration threshold, travelling separately, reuniting after arrival. Nothing is declared because nothing needs to be. 3.3.3  The point where cash meets the bank Cash is invisible until it enters or leaves the financial system, and those two moments are the only ones an institution can observe. Deposits. Cash paid in — by the customer, by third parties, at branches or through machines. Patterns worth understanding are deposits inconsistent with the customer's declared income, 41 deposits by people other than the account holder, and deposits across multiple locations that suggest more than one person is contributing. Withdrawals. Cash taken out is the more relevant direction for terrorist financing, and the less studied. A customer converting account balance to cash is removing the transaction from further observation — everything after that point is unrecorded. The withdrawal pattern that matters is not a single large sum. It is repeated moderate withdrawals over weeks, sometimes across several locations, sometimes followed by conversion into stored value. Module 1's scenario was exactly this shape, and no threshold would have caught it. The honest limitation: a withdrawal tells you money left the system. It cannot tell you where it went, and an analyst who writes a rationale implying otherwise has asserted something the record does not support. WHAT TO TAKE FROM THIS CHAPTER — Cash leaves no record, is anonymous at the point of use, works where nothing else does, and needs no infrastructure. — Several terrorist income streams are cash by nature and never need to enter banking at all. — Recommendation 32 requires border declaration systems, powers to stop and restrain, and confiscation where TF is involved. — Declaration thresholds are typically around ten thousand — far above the cost of most attacks. — Division among several couriers below the threshold defeats declaration entirely, with nothing to declare. — The only observable moments are entry and exit from the financial system. — Withdrawal is the more relevant direction for TF and the less studied — and it tells you money left, not where it went. 42
