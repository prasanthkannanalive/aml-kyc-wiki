Freezing without delay The phrase appears in Recommendation 6 and in every national implementation of it, and it means something more demanding than it sounds. This chapter sets out what the obligation requires, what freezing is and is not, and what an institution must have in place to meet it. 2.3.1  What 'without delay' means FATF's interpretive note is explicit: without delay means, ideally, within a matter of hours of a designation. It does not mean within a working day, on the next list refresh, or as soon as operationally convenient. The reasoning is stated in the note and is worth holding, because it explains the severity. The purpose of the requirement is to prevent the flight or dissipation of funds. A designation is public the moment it is made, and a designated party who learns of it before their assets are frozen has both motive and opportunity to move them. Two practical requirements follow from this that institutions often under-build. The list update cycle must be fast. A daily overnight refresh means a designation made at nine in the morning is not screened against until the following day — twenty-four hours in which the obligation is live and unmet. Terrorism designations warrant intra-day loading in a way that some other list types do not. And there must be a path for the exceptional case. When a major designation is announced, an institution needs a way to load it immediately rather than waiting for the scheduled cycle. That path should exist before it is needed, because the day it is needed is not the day to design it. 2.3.2  What freezing is Freezing means the assets are immobilised: no movement, transfer, alteration, use, access or dealing that would change their volume, amount, location, ownership, possession or character. Three things it is not, and each is a real error. It is not confiscation. Title remains with the designated party. The institution holds the assets as a custodian; it does not acquire them and the state does not take them. It is not account closure. Closing the account and returning the balance is the opposite of freezing — it releases the funds. It is not returning the funds to the sender. This is the most common failure and it is worth being emphatic about. An inbound payment for a designated beneficiary that has come into your possession must be frozen and reported. Returning it to the originating institution is a prohibited dealing, it puts the money back into circulation, and it destroys the record the authorities need. The obligation also extends beyond cash. Funds and other assets covers securities, property, vehicles, insurance policies, and anything of value — and it covers assets owned or controlled by the designated party, which is why an entity they control is caught even where it is not itself listed. 2.3.3  The making-available prohibition The second half of Recommendation 6 is separate from freezing and is breached differently. 29 No funds or other assets may be made available, directly or indirectly, to or for the benefit of a designated person. You do not need to touch their account to breach this; you need only provide something they benefit from. Indirectly is where the work is. Paying an intermediary who will pass funds on. Settling a designated person's debt to a third party. Providing services — legal, accounting, transport, telecommunications — that benefit them. Paying a family member for their household expenses. The benefit test is factual rather than formal, and it cannot be automated. A screening engine sees the immediate counterparty; it cannot see who ultimately gains. That is why this obligation depends on an analyst noticing a relationship rather than on a system matching a name. One case worth naming because it is frequently misunderstood: giving a designated person the use of an asset is making it available even where ownership never moves. Allowing them to occupy a property or draw on a facility is caught. 2.3.4  Reporting and what follows Freezing is not the end of the obligation. Every national implementation requires the institution to report frozen assets, and usually to report attempted transactions as well. The recipients differ by jurisdiction — a financial intelligence unit, a treasury department, a dedicated sanctions authority — and the deadlines are short. An analyst should know which body and which deadline applies in each jurisdiction their institution operates in, because the answer is not the same everywhere. Internal notification matters as much as external. The MLRO or equivalent, the relevant business line, and where the party is a customer, whoever owns the relationship. A frozen customer account is a relationship event as well as a compliance one. And the customer is not told why. Alerting a designated party — or someone connected to them — that their assets have been frozen for a terrorism reason risks the very dissipation the without-delay rule exists to prevent, and in most jurisdictions raises tipping-off exposure of its own. Module 5 covers this in full. The short form: the analyst does not decide what the customer is told. SCENARIO — TWENTY-FOUR HOURS A designation is announced at 09:15 on a Tuesday. Your institution loads sanctions lists once daily at 02:00. At 11:40 on Tuesday a customer instructs a transfer of €12,000 to an account in the name of the newly designated individual. The payment is screened against Monday's list, returns no hit, and executes. The designation loads at 02:00 on Wednesday and the account is frozen at 02:15. Question.  Was the obligation met? Answer.  No, and it is worth being precise about which part failed. The freeze itself was applied promptly once the list was current — 02:15 is minutes after loading. The failure is upstream: a daily cycle guarantees a window of up to twenty-four hours in which a live designation is unscreened, and on this occasion €12,000 left during it. That is not an analyst error and no individual did anything wrong; it is a control design that cannot meet a requirement stated in hours. The remedy is a faster cycle for terrorism designations specifically, plus an expedited path for announced designations, and a documented retrospective check of payments made during any window where a designation was live and unloaded. That last step is what would have surfaced this payment on Wednesday morning rather than at an inspection two years later. 30 WHAT TO TAKE FROM THIS CHAPTER — Without delay means within hours of designation — not the next refresh, not the next working day. — The purpose is to prevent flight of funds, and a designation is public the moment it is made. — A daily list cycle leaves a window of up to twenty-four hours in which the obligation is live and unmet. — Freezing immobilises. It is not confiscation, not account closure, and emphatically not returning the funds. — Returning an inbound payment to the sender is a prohibited dealing that also destroys the record. — Making available is a separate obligation, breached without touching the designated party's account. — Benefit is a factual test a screening engine cannot perform — it depends on an analyst noticing a relationship. 31
