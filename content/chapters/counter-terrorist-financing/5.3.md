# Tipping off

The prohibition on disclosure is stricter in terrorist financing than almost anywhere else in compliance, and it constrains ordinary operational behaviour in ways that are easy to breach without intending to.


**5.3.1** What the prohibition covers It is an offence in essentially every jurisdiction to disclose that a report has been made, or that an investigation is being or may be conducted, where the disclosure is likely to prejudice it. The scope is wider than analysts assume. It is not limited to saying 'we have filed a report'. It covers any disclosure likely to prejudice — which includes conduct as well as words, and includes disclosure to people other than the subject. The rationale is directly connected to Module 2's without-delay rule. A subject who learns they are under scrutiny has motive and opportunity to move funds, destroy records or alter behaviour, and in a terrorism context to accelerate whatever was planned. Two features specific to TF are worth naming. The prohibition frequently extends to disclosure to other group entities or other institutions in ways that laundering provisions may not. And the consequence of breach is more serious, because what is prejudiced may be an active investigation into an imminent act.


**5.3.2** The operational constraints The prohibition bites hardest in ordinary interactions, and these are the situations where breaches actually occur. The customer asks why a payment was delayed. The permissible answer refers to routine checks and does not name a reason. It does not say a report was made, does not say the counterparty is the issue, and does not say the delay concerns terrorism. The customer asks why their account was closed. Most jurisdictions permit closure without explanation, and where a report exists an explanation is usually prohibited. The relationship manager's instinct to give the customer a reason is exactly the instinct that breaches this. A colleague asks. Internal disclosure to people with a legitimate need is permitted; disclosure to a relationship manager who then reassures the customer is not. In practice the safe rule is that operational staff are told a payment is under review and nothing more. The customer's counterparty asks. An institution returning or delaying a payment may be asked by the sending bank for a reason, and the answer must not disclose the basis. And a group entity asks. Group-wide information sharing is normal and permitted in many contexts, and TF provisions are frequently narrower. This is one to check rather than assume.


**5.3.3** Who decides what is said The single practical rule for an analyst is that they do not decide. The MLRO or nominated officer determines what may be communicated, to whom, and when. Where an authority is involved, that authority may give directions, and those directions govern. This is not bureaucratic caution. The analyst usually does not know what else is happening — whether another institution has reported, whether an investigation is live, whether the account is being monitored deliberately. A disclosure that seems harmless from inside one file may prejudice something the analyst cannot see. Practically, three things follow. Escalate the question rather than answering it. Record that the customer asked, because the asking is itself information. And ensure operational staff have a script, since the breach usually happens at a counter or on a telephone rather than in a compliance function.


## What to take from this chapter
- It is an offence to disclose that a report has been made or that an investigation may be conducted, where that is likely to prejudice it
- The prohibition covers conduct as well as words, and disclosure to people other than the subject
- In TF it frequently extends to other group entities in ways laundering provisions may not
- The permissible answer to a delayed payment refers to routine checks and names no reason
- Most jurisdictions permit closure without explanation, and where a report exists an explanation is usually prohibited
- The analyst does not decide what is said. The MLRO does, and any authority's directions govern
- Record that the customer asked — the asking is itself information.
