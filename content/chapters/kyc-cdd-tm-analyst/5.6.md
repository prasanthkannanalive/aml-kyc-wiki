# Evidence gathering and case documentation

An AML investigation is only as good as its documentation. The case file is the product — it is what survives when the analyst moves on, what the examiner reviews, and what the investigator or prosecutor relies on. Evidence gathering is not an afterthought; it is the discipline that makes the rest of the work defensible.

## What counts as evidence

In AML work, evidence is broader than in criminal investigation:

- **Transaction records** — the payments, amounts, dates, counterparties, channels.
- **KYC documents** — identity verification, corporate records, beneficial ownership evidence.
- **Customer communications** — RFIs sent, responses received, explanations provided.
- **System records** — alerts generated, rules triggered, dispositions made.
- **External checks** — sanctions screening results, adverse media findings, OSINT sources.
- **Analyst work product** — notes, timelines, network maps, assessments.

Every piece must be preserved with provenance: where it came from, when it was obtained, and who obtained it.

## The case file structure

A well-organised case file typically contains:

- **Alert summary** — what triggered the investigation, which rule or typology.
- **Customer profile** — KYC data, risk rating, expected activity.
- **Transaction analysis** — the specific transactions, the pattern, the narrative.
- **Investigative steps** — what was checked, what was found, what was ruled out.
- **Counterparty analysis** — who the other parties are, their risk, their connections.
- **RFI record** — what was requested, what was received, assessment of response.
- **Disposition** — the decision, the rationale, the escalation if any.
- **SAR/STR** — if filed, the report and filing confirmation.

## Documentation standards

- **Contemporaneous** — record as you go, not reconstructed later from memory.
- **Factual** — state what was found, not what you assumed.
- **Complete** — include dead ends and ruled-out hypotheses; they show the work was done.
- **Consistent** — use the same terminology, dates and references throughout.
- **Preserved** — do not alter or delete records; corrections are made by amendment, not erasure.

## The audit trail

Every action in the investigation should be traceable: who did what, when, and why. System logs, email records, document management metadata and analyst notes all contribute. An examiner should be able to reconstruct the investigation from the file alone.

## Common documentation failures

- Relying on memory instead of contemporaneous notes.
- Recording conclusions without the supporting facts.
- Missing the "why" — what was checked and what was ruled out.
- Inconsistent dates, amounts or references across documents.
- Failing to preserve the customer's actual responses vs paraphrased summaries.

## Takeaways

- Evidence = transactions + KYC + communications + system records + external checks + analyst work.
- Case file structure: alert → profile → analysis → steps → counterparties → RFI → disposition → SAR.
- Document contemporaneously, factually, completely, consistently, preserved.
- The audit trail must let an examiner reconstruct the investigation.
- Dead ends and ruled-out hypotheses are evidence of work done — include them.
