# Unresolved structures: documentation and escalation

Some customer structures cannot be fully understood — not because the analyst did not try hard enough, but because the structure is designed to resist understanding. This chapter covers what to do when beneficial ownership cannot be established, and why "we tried" is not a sufficient answer on its own.

## When structures cannot be resolved

Legitimate reasons a structure may not resolve:

- **Bearer shares** still outstanding — ownership genuinely unknowable.
- **Secrecy jurisdiction refusals** — the registry or trustee will not provide information.
- **Complex trust arrangements** — beneficiaries defined as a class, protector powers unclear.
- **Stale or unavailable records** — company dissolved, records lost, jurisdiction uncooperative.
- **Customer non-cooperation** — the customer cannot or will not provide the documents.

Illegitimate reasons — and more common:

- The analyst did not ask the right questions.
- The analyst accepted the customer's explanation without verification.
- The analyst ran out of time and moved on.
- The analyst did not know what to ask for.

## The FATF standard: reasonable efforts

FATF Recommendation 24 requires countries to ensure that legal persons cannot be misused for ML/TF, and that competent authorities can obtain adequate, accurate and timely BO information. For the firm, the standard is reasonable efforts to identify BO — documented, good-faith attempts, not perfection.

Where BO cannot be identified despite reasonable efforts, most regimes require:

- **Enhanced scrutiny** — more documentation, more verification, more senior oversight.
- **Documented analysis** — what was tried, what failed, why.
- **Risk-based decision** — whether to onboard, onboard with restrictions, or decline.

## The escalation path

When a structure cannot be resolved:

1. **Document everything** — every document requested, every registry checked, every party identified, every gap.
2. **Escalate to compliance** — unresolved BO is not an analyst-level decision.
3. **Apply enhanced controls** — if onboarding proceeds, monitoring must be tighter, review more frequent, and the file flagged as unresolved.
4. **Consider declining** — some structures carry risk that cannot be mitigated. Declining is a control.
5. **Review periodically** — unresolved structures should be re-examined; jurisdictions change, registers open, information emerges.

## What examiners look for

- Did the firm make documented reasonable efforts?
- Was the failure escalated and decided at the right level?
- Were enhanced controls applied to unresolved-BO customers?
- Is there a pattern — the same formation agents, the same jurisdictions, the same opaque structures recurring?

A single unresolved structure may be manageable; a portfolio of them is a business-model problem.

## Takeaways

- Unresolved BO requires documented reasonable efforts — what was tried, what failed, why.
- Escalate to compliance; do not decide alone.
- Apply enhanced controls if onboarding proceeds; consider declining.
- Track patterns — recurring opaque structures from the same sources are a portfolio-level risk.
- Re-review unresolved structures periodically; things change.
