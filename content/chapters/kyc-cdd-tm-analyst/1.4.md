# The customer lifecycle: onboarding to exit

AML is not an onboarding exercise — it is a lifecycle. A customer enters, is monitored, is reviewed, and eventually exits or is exited. Each stage has its own controls, and the handoffs between stages are where programmes break.

## Stage 1: Onboarding

The gate. CDD is performed, risk is rated, sanctions are screened, and the expected-activity profile is recorded. The output is not just an account — it is a documented risk assessment and a baseline for everything that follows.

Key onboarding decisions: accept at standard CDD, accept with EDD, accept with restrictions, or decline. The decline decision is a control, not a failure — document it.

## Stage 2: Ongoing monitoring

The longest stage. Transaction monitoring watches activity against the baseline; periodic reviews refresh KYC on schedule; trigger events (PEP status change, adverse media, unusual activity, ownership change) force unscheduled refreshes.

The quality question: does monitoring actually compare activity to the recorded profile? A customer who said "occasional international wires for consulting" and then moves millions should generate alerts — if it does not, the baseline was never properly recorded or the monitoring rules were never tuned to it.

## Stage 3: Periodic review and refresh

Every risk tier has a review cycle — high-risk customers annually or more often, low-risk every few years. Reviews re-verify identity documents, confirm ownership is unchanged, refresh source of funds/wealth for EDD customers, and re-run sanctions and adverse-media checks.

The common failure: reviews that are rubber stamps — documents re-collected but never re-verified, PEP status never re-checked, risk rating never revisited. A review that cannot change the rating is not a review.

## Stage 4: Exit and offboarding

Customers leave — voluntarily, through dormancy, or because the relationship became unmanageable. Exit is an AML event:

- Dormant accounts reactivating after long inactivity need re-verification — the person returning may not be the person who opened it.
- Offboarding a high-risk or SAR-subject customer must be handled without tipping off.
- Closed accounts with residual balances or pending transactions need documented handling.

## The handoffs that fail

- Onboarding records an expected-activity profile that monitoring never uses.
- Monitoring generates alerts that investigation cannot resolve because KYC data is stale.
- Reviews happen but findings never flow back into the risk rating.
- Exit decisions are made by product teams without compliance input.

Each handoff is a documented process with an owner — in a well-run programme, no stage assumes another stage caught something.

## Takeaways

- Lifecycle = onboarding → monitoring → periodic review → exit; each stage has distinct controls.
- Onboarding produces the baseline (expected activity + risk rating) that everything else depends on.
- Reviews must be capable of changing the rating — otherwise they are administrative.
- Exit is an AML event: dormant reactivation, SAR-subject offboarding and residual balances all need handling.
- Handoffs between stages are where programmes break — document ownership at each boundary.
