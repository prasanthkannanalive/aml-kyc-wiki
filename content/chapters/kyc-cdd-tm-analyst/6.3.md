# Filing timelines across jurisdictions

SAR/STR deadlines are statutory, jurisdiction-specific and unforgiving. Missing a deadline is itself a reportable failure — and the internal escalation chain must be designed so the deadline is never at risk because an alert sat in a queue.

## United States: FinCEN SAR

- **Deadline**: 30 calendar days from the date of initial detection of facts that may constitute a basis for filing.
- **60-day extension**: when no subject can be identified, the filing deadline extends to 60 days to allow identification.
- **CTR**: Currency Transaction Report for cash transactions over $10,000 — separate from SAR, filed within 15 days.
- **Electronic filing**: mandatory through the BSA E-Filing System.
- **Continuing activity**: if suspicious activity continues after filing, file a continuing SAR at least every 90 days.

## United Kingdom: NCA SAR and the DAML regime

The UK system is more nuanced than a simple deadline — it runs on the **DAML (Defence Against Money Laundering)** mechanism under POCA 2002:

- **The obligation**: a person must report knowledge or suspicion of money laundering "as soon as practicable" — promptness is expected.
- **Two types of DAML request**:
  - **SAR (consent request)** — the reporter asks the NCA for consent to proceed with the transaction. The NCA has **7 working days** (the "notice period") to respond; if it does not, consent is **deemed** to have been given.
  - **SAR (no-consent / defence request)** — used where the transaction has already gone through or cannot be delayed; the reporter seeks the statutory defence against money-laundering offences.
- **The 31-day moratorium**: if the NCA responds within the notice period saying it needs more time, it can extend the moratorium up to **31 days** total while it investigates.
- **Deemed consent**: silence after the notice period = consent. This is the operational heart of UK SAR work — analysts must diarise the 7-working-day clock religiously.
- **Nominated officer**: the MLRO reviews and authorises filings.
- **Tipping-off**: prohibited under POCA 2002; the confidentiality is strict, though POCA does contain narrow permitted-disclosure exceptions (for example, disclosures required by law or made to the NCA itself).

The UKFIU's SAR portal guidance and Circular 022/2015 are the authoritative references for the DAML mechanics.

## European Union: FIUs and the 2024 AML package

- **Deadline**: varies by member state — typically a few days to weeks.
- **Format**: varies — some use standardised forms (goAML in several jurisdictions), others accept structured narratives.
- **6AMLD**: expanded predicate offences and harmonised definitions but left filing mechanics to member states.
- **The 2024 AML package** — the biggest EU change in a decade: a directly applicable **AMLR (Anti-Money Laundering Regulation)** creating an EU single rulebook, plus the **AMLA (Anti-Money Laundering Authority)** in Frankfurt supervising the highest-risk obliged entities directly. Transposition and application timelines run through the late 2020s — watch this space.

## India: FIU-IND STR

- **Deadline**: under PMLA Rule 8, the STR is due **within 7 days of arriving at a conclusion** that a transaction is suspicious — this applies to all reporting entities, not just certain categories.
- **Format**: prescribed STR formats for banking companies and other reporting entities.
- **Electronic filing**: through the FIU-IND portal.

## The internal deadline problem

The statutory deadline starts when the firm "detects" suspicion — which is earlier than when the analyst finishes the investigation. The internal process must account for this:

- Alert → triage → investigation → MLRO review → filing.
- Each step takes time; the deadline clock is already running.
- Escalation paths must be fast enough to meet the deadline even for complex cases.

Best practice: build internal SLAs that are shorter than the statutory deadline, so there is buffer for complex investigations.

## What happens if you miss it

- **Regulatory action** — fines, enforcement, exam findings.
- **Criminal liability** — in some jurisdictions, failure to report is an offence for the individual MLRO or compliance officer.
- **Evidence problems** — late filings may be less useful to law enforcement.
- **Reputational damage** — "the bank knew and did not report" is a headline.

## Takeaways

- Deadlines: US 30 days (60 if no subject identified), UK "as soon as practicable" via DAML, EU varies, India 7 days from conclusion of suspicion.
- UK DAML: 7-working-day NCA notice period, 31-day maximum moratorium, deemed consent on silence.
- CTR (US) is separate from SAR — cash over $10,000.
- EU 2024 AML package (AMLR + AMLA) will reshape the regime — monitor implementation.
- Internal SLAs must be shorter than statutory deadlines.
- Continuing suspicious activity requires continuing SARs (US: every 90 days).
