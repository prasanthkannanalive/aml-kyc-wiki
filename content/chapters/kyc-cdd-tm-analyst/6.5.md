# Electronic filing, forms and recordkeeping

Once the decision to file is made, the mechanics matter — wrong form, wrong format, missing fields, or late filing can all undermine an otherwise good SAR. This chapter covers the practical filing requirements across major jurisdictions.

## US FinCEN SAR: technical requirements

FinCEN SARs are filed electronically through the BSA E-Filing System:

- **Form**: FinCEN Form 111 (Suspicious Activity Report).
- **Format**: XML-based electronic filing, batch or individual.
- **Required fields**: subject information, activity details, narrative, suspicious activity types, dates, amounts, institutions involved.
- **Joint filings**: multiple institutions can file jointly for the same activity.
- **Continuing activity**: file a continuing SAR every 90 days if activity persists.

The FinCEN SAR Electronic Filing Instructions provide the technical specifications — field definitions, XML schema, validation rules. Filing errors are common: missing required fields, incorrect date formats, narrative exceeding character limits.

## UK NCA SAR: portal filing

UK SARs are submitted through the NCA online portal:

- **Format**: web-based form with structured fields + narrative.
- **Required**: subject details, activity description, grounds for suspicion.
- **No fixed form number** — the portal guides the filer through required information.
- **Nominated officer**: the MLRO reviews and authorises before submission.

## EU FIU filing

EU member states use different systems:

- Some use standardised forms (goAML in several jurisdictions).
- Others accept structured narratives or national formats.
- Deadlines and fields vary by member state.

## FIU-IND STR: prescribed formats

India requires specific STR formats for different reporting entity types:

- **Banking companies** — prescribed format under PMLA rules.
- **Other reporting entities** — different formats for NBFCs, DNFBPs, etc.
- **Electronic filing** — through the FIU-IND portal.
- **Deadlines** — typically within 7 days of suspicion for certain categories.

## Recordkeeping requirements

SAR/STR records must be retained for the statutory period — typically 5–7 years depending on jurisdiction. This includes:

- The filed report itself.
- Supporting documentation (transactions, KYC, investigation notes).
- Filing confirmation and any FIU correspondence.
- Internal approval records (MLRO sign-off).

Records must be accessible for examination — disorganised files are as bad as missing ones.

## Common filing errors

- **Incomplete narratives** — the "why suspicious" is vague or missing.
- **Wrong subject identification** — name variations, missing identifiers, wrong account numbers.
- **Date errors** — activity dates vs filing dates confused.
- **Missing continuing SARs** — activity continues but no follow-up filed.
- **Late filing** — deadline missed because internal process was too slow.

## Takeaways

- US: FinCEN Form 111 via BSA E-Filing; UK: NCA portal; EU: varies; India: FIU-IND prescribed formats.
- Required fields vary but all need: who, what, when, where, why, how.
- Recordkeeping: 5–7 years typical; include report, supporting docs, confirmations, approvals.
- Common errors: incomplete narratives, wrong identifiers, date confusion, missed continuing SARs, late filing.
- Test filings before you need them — know the system before the deadline.
