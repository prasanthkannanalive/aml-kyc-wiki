# RCAs: relatives and close associates

PEP risk does not stop at the officeholder. The relatives and close associates — RCAs — of a PEP are treated as PEPs themselves in most regimes, because the official's influence and access to public resources can be exercised through the people around them.

## Who counts as an RCA

The FATF definition covers two categories:

**Relatives** — the family members most likely to be used as conduits. The exact list is regime-specific, so check your rules: under UK MLR the family members are the spouse or civil partner, children (and their spouses/civil partners) and parents; "siblings" are not automatically RCAs under UK rules, though FATF guidance and some other regimes take a broader view. Where the regime is silent, treat close family as in scope on a risk basis.

**Close associates** — the people the PEP does business or politics with:
- Business partners and joint venture associates
- Senior advisors, chiefs of staff, campaign managers
- People known to be close personal friends
- Entities in which the PEP or their family holds significant ownership or control

The test is not just legal relationship — it is whether the person is in a position to benefit from the PEP's influence, or to act on their behalf.

## Why RCAs matter

The RCA is where PEP risk actually hides. A minister will rarely open a personal account showing unexplained wealth — but their spouse's import-export company, their child's property purchases, or their business partner's consulting firm might. These entities and individuals can be used to:

- Move bribe proceeds or embezzled funds.
- Park unexplained wealth at arm's length from the official.
- Access banking services the official themselves might not qualify for.

## Identifying RCAs

Sources for RCA identification:
- Adverse media — news reports naming family members and associates.
- Corporate records — companies where the PEP or family members are directors or shareholders.
- Social and professional networks — LinkedIn, business registries, political donations.
- Customer disclosures — the onboarding form asks about PEP relationships; watch for evasive answers.
- PEP database providers — most commercial databases flag RCAs where they can identify them.

The hard truth: RCA identification is imperfect. You cannot always know who a PEP's close associates are, especially in opaque jurisdictions. The control is reasonable effort + documented analysis, not omniscience.

## EDD for RCAs

An RCA customer gets the same EDD treatment as the PEP: source of funds and source of wealth, senior approval, enhanced monitoring. The difference is that the link to the PEP must also be documented — who the PEP is, what the relationship is, and why the customer is being treated as PEP-linked.

## Takeaways

- RCAs = regime-defined relatives (UK: spouse/civil partner, children + partners, parents) + close associates (business partners, advisors, entities they control).
- RCAs inherit PEP risk because they can be conduits for the official's influence or unexplained wealth.
- Identification sources: adverse media, corporate records, social/professional networks, customer disclosures, PEP databases.
- RCA customers get full EDD + documented link to the PEP.
- You cannot always identify every RCA — the standard is reasonable documented effort.
