# Digital ID, liveness checks and biometrics

As onboarding moves remote, identity verification has shifted from document inspection to digital identity proofing. The FATF's 2020 Digital ID guidance recognises that robust digital ID systems can actually exceed paper-based KYC — but only if the firm understands what the technology proves and what it does not.

## The digital identity stack

Modern remote verification typically combines:

- **Document capture** — the customer photographs their ID; OCR extracts the data.
- **Chip verification** — for e-passports and e-ID cards, the NFC chip is read to confirm the document data is authentic and matches the visual inspection.
- **Liveness detection** — the customer performs an action (blink, turn head, read a code) to prove a live human is present, not a photo, video or mask.
- **Facial comparison** — the selfie is matched against the portrait on the document and/or against a biometric database.
- **Database checks** — the declared identity is checked against credit bureaus, government databases or utility records.

Each layer covers a different attack: document fraud, replay attacks, presentation attacks (masks, deepfakes), and identity theft.

## What can go wrong

- **Deepfakes and injection attacks** — sophisticated fakes can defeat weaker liveness checks; the technology is an arms race.
- **Document fraud** — high-quality fake documents pass OCR if the security features are not verified.
- **Biometric database gaps** — a person with no credit history or digital footprint may fail checks designed for the digitally active.
- **Accessibility and fairness** — verification systems can disadvantage people with disabilities, older documents, or those in low-connectivity regions.
- **Over-reliance** — a green "verified" result is a starting point, not a conclusion; the risk assessment still applies.

## Digital ID and the risk-based approach

The FATF guidance is explicit: the reliability of a digital ID system should be proportionate to the risk. A low-risk customer may be adequately verified by a basic document + selfie check; a high-risk customer may need in-person verification, video KYC with an agent, or additional corroboration.

## The analyst's role

Even in fully automated flows, analysts handle exceptions: failed liveness checks, mismatched biometrics, documents from high-fraud jurisdictions, customers who cannot complete digital verification. These exceptions are not failures of the system — they are the system's designed escalation path. The quality question is whether exceptions are investigated properly or rubber-stamped through.

## Takeaways

- Digital ID stacks combine document capture, chip verification, liveness, facial match and database checks — each covering a different attack.
- No single check is sufficient; the stack's strength is in the combination.
- Verification depth should match risk — low-risk can use lighter checks, high-risk needs more.
- Exceptions (failed checks, unusual documents) are the analyst's job — investigate, don't bypass.
