# Unwrapping multi-layered holding companies

The hardest part of KYB is not verifying the customer in front of you — it is unwrapping the structure behind them. A holding company owned by another holding company owned by a trust is not an edge case; it is the standard architecture of serious wealth and serious crime alike.

## Why structures are layered

Layering serves legitimate purposes: tax efficiency, asset protection, estate planning, regulatory arbitrage. It also serves illicit ones: obscuring beneficial ownership, breaking the audit trail, separating the person from the money. The AML analyst's job is not to assume the worst — it is to determine whether the structure can be understood and whether the people behind it can be identified.

## The unwrapping process

For each layer, the analyst needs:

- **Corporate registry extract** — the official record showing shareholders, directors, registered address, company status.
- **Ownership percentage** — who owns what, directly and indirectly.
- **Control analysis** — who can appoint directors, sign documents, access accounts — regardless of share percentage.
- **Jurisdiction assessment** — where each entity is incorporated and whether that jurisdiction cooperates with information requests.

Each hop generates two questions: who owns this entity, and who controls it? A 10% shareholder with board control may matter more than a 40% passive investor.

## Common structures and how to read them

- **Simple holding** — Parent owns OpCo which does business. Easy: identify the parent's owners.
- **Multi-jurisdictional chain** — UK HoldCo → BVI Sub → Cayman LP → individual. Each hop needs a registry check; the chain ends when you reach natural persons or hit a jurisdiction that will not disclose.
- **Cross-shareholdings** — Company A owns B, B owns A. Circular ownership makes percentage calculation genuinely hard — map it, do not guess.
- **Nominee arrangements** — a nominee shareholder or director holds on behalf of someone else. The nominee is not the beneficial owner; the underlying principal is.

## When the chain does not resolve

Some structures legitimately cannot be fully unwrapped — bearer shares, nominee arrangements in secrecy jurisdictions, trusts where the beneficial owners are not disclosed. The FATF guidance is clear: the firm must make reasonable efforts to identify BO, and where it cannot, it must apply enhanced scrutiny, document the failure, and consider whether the business relationship should proceed at all.

"We tried and could not determine the beneficial owner" is a documented outcome — not a reason to stop looking, and not an excuse to onboard without understanding why the structure is opaque.

## Takeaways

- Each layer needs registry extract + ownership % + control analysis + jurisdiction check.
- Ownership and control are separate questions — answer both at every hop.
- Circular ownership, nominees and secrecy jurisdictions are the hard cases — map them, don't guess.
- Unresolvable structures need documented good-faith effort + enhanced controls or decline.
