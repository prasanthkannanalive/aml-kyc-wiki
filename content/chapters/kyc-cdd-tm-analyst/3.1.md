# Entity Verification and Corporate Registry Checks

Onboarding a company is where CDD gets genuinely hard. A person walks in with a passport; a company arrives with a registry extract, a board resolution, a web of shareholders, nominees, trusts and holding companies — and the person sitting in front of you may not own or control anything at all.

## KYB: know your business

Business onboarding adds a layer on top of CDD. Before serving a legal entity, the firm must verify:

- **Legal existence** — the entity is registered, in good standing, and authorised to operate.
- **Legal form and powers** — what kind of entity it is, and who can legally bind it (directors, partners, authorised signatories).
- **Registered details** — registered address, incorporation documents, tax identifiers where applicable.
- **The people behind it** — ownership and control, which is where UBO work begins.

Corporate registry checks are the backbone. In most jurisdictions the registry is the authoritative source for existence and basic officers; analysts should verify against the official register where possible, not just documents the customer hands over.

## Ultimate beneficial ownership

A beneficial owner is the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the entity — FATF Recommendation 24 does not itself set a percentage — the 25% figure commonly used comes from interpretive guidance and national implementations (UK/EU/US all use 25%), while some regimes set it lower. Control-based identification is required regardless of percentage.

The practical problem is chains. A customer may be owned by Company A, which is owned by Company B in another jurisdiction, which is owned by a trust whose settlor is a PEP. Each hop needs documentation: registry extracts, shareholder registers, partnership agreements, trust deeds.

Key rules of thumb:

- **Follow the chain until you reach natural persons.** A corporate shareholder is not an answer — it is the next question.
- **Control counts as much as ownership.** Someone who can appoint or remove directors, or who has significant influence, may be a beneficial owner even below the threshold.
- **Trusts and foundations need special handling.** Identify the settlor, trustees, protectors, beneficiaries and any person with effective control — the structure exists precisely to obscure this.
- **Nominees and bearer shares are red flags.** Bearer shares, where still permitted, make ownership unverifiable by design; their presence should trigger enhanced scrutiny or refusal.

## US BOI registry — current state (2026)

US corporate-transparency rules have shifted twice recently, and analysts must not confuse two separate obligations:

- **The CTA/BOI registry (FinCEN):** the March 2025 interim final rule exempted domestic companies from BOI reporting; the August 2026 final rule made that permanent — **US companies and US persons are exempt**, while **foreign reporting companies remain in scope**. For most US-domiciled business customers this means there is no BOI filing to check against.
- **The bank-side CDD Rule is unaffected.** FinCEN's Customer Due Diligence Rule still requires US banks to identify and verify beneficial owners of legal entity customers — **25%+ owners plus one control person** — regardless of BOI registry status. The registry's exemption does not weaken your KYB obligations; it just means you cannot rely on a central BOI database for domestic entities and must collect ownership information directly.

## Red flags in entity onboarding

Experienced analysts watch for:

- Ownership structures that cannot be fully documented or verified.
- Recent, unexplained changes to ownership or directors.
- Entities registered in secrecy jurisdictions with no apparent business reason.
- Businesses whose stated activity does not match their jurisdiction, size or transaction patterns.
- Requests to bank for a third party's benefit with no clear commercial rationale.
- Complex structures where the customer cannot explain who ultimately benefits.

## Documenting the work

KYB files fail on documentation, not effort. For every entity customer the file should show: registry verification performed, ownership chain mapped, each UBO identified with verification evidence, control persons identified, and the risk rationale for any structure that could not be fully resolved.

When ownership cannot be established despite reasonable effort, most regimes do not require infinite digging — they require documented good faith, escalation, and often a decision not to onboard or to apply the strongest available controls.

## Takeaways

- KYB = verifying legal existence, powers, registered details and the people behind the entity.
- Trace ownership to natural persons; corporate shareholders are questions, not answers.
- Control and influence count alongside ownership percentage; trusts need settlor/trustee/beneficiary mapping.
- Bearer shares, nominee arrangements and unexplainable complexity are escalation triggers.
- Document the chain and the risk rationale — an unexplained structure is worse than a declined customer.
