# UBO thresholds: 10% vs 25% and indirect ownership

The single most-asked technical question in KYB: at what percentage does someone become a beneficial owner? The honest answer is that it depends on the regime — and the difference between 10% and 25% thresholds changes who you must identify.

## The threshold landscape

Different regimes set different thresholds:

- **FATF Recommendation 24** does not set a specific percentage — it requires countries to identify the natural persons who ultimately own or control legal persons, with a threshold-based approach as one option.
- **EU AMLD** uses 25% as the standard beneficial ownership threshold for corporate entities (with a 25% threshold for trusts' settlors/beneficiaries in some interpretations).
- **UK MLR 2017** uses 25% for corporate beneficial owners, with additional "significant influence or control" criteria that can capture people below the threshold.
- **US FinCEN CDD Rule** requires identifying individuals who own 25% or more of legal entity customers, plus one control person.
- **Some jurisdictions** use 10% or even lower for higher-risk sectors or entity types.

The practical rule: check your jurisdiction's threshold, but understand that ownership percentage is only one way to be a beneficial owner.

## Control-based beneficial ownership

Many regimes — including the UK and FATF-aligned frameworks — recognise that someone can be a beneficial owner through control even below the ownership threshold:

- Power to appoint or remove directors.
- Power to amend the constitutional documents.
- Significant influence over strategic decisions.
- Control through voting rights, options, or other arrangements.

A 10% shareholder who controls the board is a beneficial owner in many regimes. A 30% shareholder with purely passive investment rights may not be — check the control criteria, not just the percentage.

## Calculating indirect ownership

When ownership is layered, the calculation multiplies through the chain:

- Person owns 50% of HoldCo A.
- HoldCo A owns 60% of OpCo B.
- The person's indirect ownership of OpCo B = 50% × 60% = 30%.

This is direct multiplication for simple chains. But watch for:
- **Multiple paths** — the same person may own OpCo through several chains; sum the indirect percentages.
- **Cross-shareholdings** — circular ownership requires careful algebra, not guesswork.
- **Threshold comparison** — compare the calculated indirect percentage against the applicable threshold.

## The control-person requirement

The US FinCEN CDD rule adds a specific requirement beyond ownership: identify at least one "control person" — the individual with significant responsibility to control, manage or direct the entity — regardless of ownership percentage. This catches the CEO or managing director who owns nothing but runs everything.

## Practical approach

1. Determine the applicable threshold for your jurisdiction and entity type.
2. Map the ownership chain and calculate indirect percentages.
3. Identify control persons separately from owners.
4. Document the calculation — show the chain, the percentages, the threshold applied.
5. Where ownership cannot be calculated (opaque structures), apply the control-based and reasonable-effort standards.

## Takeaways

- Thresholds vary: 25% (UK/EU/US standard), 10% or lower in some regimes — check yours.
- Control-based ownership captures people below the threshold — check both.
- Indirect ownership multiplies through chains; multiple paths sum.
- Control persons (managers/directors) may be required separately from owners.
- Document the calculation — the chain, percentages and threshold applied.
