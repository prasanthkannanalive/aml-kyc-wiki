# Rapid movement of funds and funnel accounts

The fastest way to launder money is to move it before anyone notices — and transaction monitoring is specifically designed to catch exactly that. Rapid movement of funds, pass-through accounts and funnel accounts are the layering stage made visible in the data.

## What rapid movement looks like

The pattern is simple: money arrives, then leaves quickly — often within hours or days — with little or no legitimate purpose for the holding period.

- **Pass-through accounts** — receive funds and forward them almost immediately, keeping only a small spread or fee.
- **Velocity alerts** — incoming transfer followed within hours by outgoing transfer of similar amount.
- **Layering chains** — funds move through multiple accounts or institutions in rapid succession, each hop adding a layer of separation.

The FinCEN funnel-account advisory (FIN-2014-A005) describes the extreme version: accounts that exist primarily to receive and disburse funds for third parties, often with no other apparent business purpose.

## Why rapid movement matters

Speed is the launderer's advantage — the less time money sits in an account, the less opportunity for detection, freeze or recall. Rapid movement also breaks the audit trail: by the time an alert is investigated, the money has moved on through multiple jurisdictions and entities.

## Detection signals

- **Time between credits and debits** — hours or days, not weeks or months.
- **Amount correlation** — outgoing amounts closely match incoming (minus fees).
- **Account dormancy** — previously inactive accounts suddenly active.
- **No commensurate activity** — the account's other activity (if any) does not explain the pass-through pattern.
- **Counterparty concentration** — funds consistently come from and go to the same or related parties.

## Funnel accounts specifically

Funnel accounts are a distinct typology: they are used to collect funds from multiple sources and redistribute them — often unwittingly by the account holder (money mules) or deliberately by criminal networks. Signs:

- Multiple deposits from unrelated sources.
- Rapid disbursement to a small number of recipients.
- Account holder has no apparent business reason for the activity.
- The account was recently opened or dormant.

## Investigation approach

For a rapid-movement alert:

1. Map the flow — who sent, who received, how fast, how much retained.
2. Check the counterparty — is the sender/receiver known, related, or high-risk?
3. Review account history — is this a one-off or a pattern?
4. Check for mule indicators — was the account holder recruited, compensated, or deceived?
5. Assess whether funds reached their final destination or are still moving.

## Takeaways

- Rapid movement = placement followed quickly by layering; detection = velocity + amount correlation.
- Pass-through accounts keep little; funnel accounts collect and redistribute for third parties.
- Key signals: time between in/out, amount matching, dormancy, counterparty concentration, no business purpose.
- Investigation must trace the flow before funds move on — speed is the enemy.
