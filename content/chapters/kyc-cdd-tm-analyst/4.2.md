# Thresholds, tuning and false-positive management

The alert queue is the transaction monitoring system's most important output — and the most misunderstood. A monitoring programme is not measured by how many alerts it generates but by how accurately it separates suspicious activity from normal business. Thresholds and tuning are where that accuracy is won or lost.

## Setting thresholds that work

A threshold is the trigger point for an alert. Set it wrong and the system either floods with noise or misses real activity:

- **Too low** — every customer alerts constantly; analysts learn to ignore the queue; real alerts get lost in false positives.
- **Too high** — suspicious activity passes under the radar; the system generates few alerts but misses the ones that matter.
- **Right** — alerts fire on activity that deviates meaningfully from the customer's profile or from known typologies.

Thresholds should be set per customer segment, not globally — a threshold appropriate for a corporate treasury client is wrong for a retail customer. The best thresholds reflect the customer's expected activity, not a round number picked once.

## The tuning cycle

Tuning is not a one-time setup — it is continuous:

1. **Baseline** — set initial thresholds based on typology knowledge and customer segments.
2. **Measure** — track alert volumes, false-positive rates, SAR conversion rates per rule.
3. **Adjust** — raise thresholds on rules generating noise, lower them on rules missing known typologies.
4. **Document** — every change gets a rationale: what was changed, why, expected impact.
5. **Review** — periodic review of all rules, especially those never touched.

Examiners specifically ask for tuning history — a rule unchanged for years is a finding.

## False-positive management

False positives are not failures — they are the cost of sensitivity. The goal is to manage them, not eliminate them:

- **Whitelist legitimate patterns** — known payroll runs, regular supplier payments, seasonal spikes.
- **Segment thresholds** — different limits for different customer types.
- **Time-window tuning** — structuring rules should look at rolling windows, not single days.
- **Feedback loops** — analyst dispositions should feed back into rule tuning; if analysts consistently close an alert type as false positive, the rule needs adjustment.

## The metrics that matter

- **Alert volume per rule** — is it stable, growing, or spiking?
- **False-positive rate** — what percentage of alerts are closed as false positives?
- **SAR conversion rate** — what percentage of investigated alerts become reports?
- **Time to disposition** — are alerts being cleared within SLA?

A rule with a 95% false-positive rate is not working — it is generating work without value.

## Takeaways

- Thresholds must reflect customer expected activity, not arbitrary round numbers.
- Tuning is continuous: baseline → measure → adjust → document → review.
- False positives are managed through whitelists, segmentation and feedback loops — not ignored.
- Track alert volume, FP rate, SAR conversion and disposition time per rule.
- A rule never reviewed in years is an examiner finding.
