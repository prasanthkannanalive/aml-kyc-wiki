# Source of Wealth vs Source of Funds

Two of the most important — and most confused — concepts in enhanced due diligence are Source of Funds (SoF) and Source of Wealth (SoW). Analysts who cannot distinguish them produce weak EDD files that fail review.

## Source of Funds: where did this money come from?

SoF is specific and transactional: the origin of the particular funds being deposited or transacted. If a customer deposits €50,000, SoF asks: where did that €50,000 come from? Salary, business revenue, property sale, inheritance, investment returns, a loan.

SoF is usually verified with documents: payslips, tax returns, sale contracts, bank statements showing the accumulation, gift letters, loan agreements. It is the "show me the money trail" question.

## Source of Wealth: how did they get rich?

SoW is broader and historical: how did the customer accumulate their overall wealth over their lifetime? A PEP who earns an official salary of $80,000 but lives in a $5M property and drives a supercar has a SoW question that SoF cannot answer.

SoW verification is harder: it requires understanding the customer's career, business interests, asset accumulation, family wealth, and whether the wealth trajectory matches their known income. For PEPs especially, SoW is where unexplained enrichment shows up — the official who was modest on appointment and is wealthy a few years later.

## Why both matter

- **SoF alone is insufficient** — a customer can show legitimate income (SoF) while their total wealth vastly exceeds what that income could produce (SoW fails).
- **SoW alone is insufficient** — a wealthy customer can have legitimate wealth (SoW) but deposit funds from a suspicious specific source (SoF fails).
- **Together they bracket the risk** — SoF explains the transaction, SoW explains the customer.

## Verification approaches

For individuals: employment history, business ownership records, property records, investment portfolios, inheritance documentation, tax filings.

For PEPs: the Wolfsberg SoW/SoF FAQs emphasise corroboration — do not just accept the customer's explanation; cross-check against public records, asset registries, business filings, and adverse media. A PEP's wealth should be explainable by their known career and assets, not just by their say-so.

## Red flags

- Wealth disproportionate to known income or career.
- Reluctance to explain SoW, or explanations that change between requests.
- Funds from sources inconsistent with the customer's profile.
- Third-party funding with no clear relationship or commercial rationale.

## Takeaways

- SoF = origin of specific funds (transactional, document-backed). SoW = how total wealth was accumulated (historical, career-level).
- Both are required for EDD — SoF explains the deposit, SoW explains the customer.
- SoW is where PEP unexplained enrichment typically surfaces.
- Corroborate, do not just accept — cross-check against public records and adverse media.
