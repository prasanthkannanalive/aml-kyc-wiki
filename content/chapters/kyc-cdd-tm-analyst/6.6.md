# Law enforcement requests and production orders

Filing a SAR is not the end of the AML process — it is often the beginning. Law enforcement and financial intelligence units may follow up with requests for information, documents, or account freezes. Understanding how these requests work is essential for compliance teams.

## Types of law enforcement requests

- **Information requests** — FIU or law enforcement asks for specific transaction records, customer information, or investigation details.
- **Production orders** — court orders requiring the institution to produce documents or information.
- **Account freezes / restraints** — requests to freeze accounts suspected of criminal activity. In the UK these normally flow from a **refused DAML** or a court restraint order — not a police "request"; understand which instrument you are actually holding.
- **Search warrants** — law enforcement physically searches premises for documents or data.
- **International requests** — requests from foreign FIUs or law enforcement via mutual legal assistance treaties (MLATs).

## Jurisdiction-specific mechanics

The instruments differ by country — an analyst should know which regime they are operating under:

- **United Kingdom:** production can be compelled by court order (for example under PACE 1984 for evidence, or POCA 2002 restraint and recovery powers for proceeds). Account freezes typically arise from a refused DAML consent request or a Crown Court restraint order — the institution does not freeze merely on a police request.
- **United States:** prosecutors can issue **grand jury subpoenas** and search warrants; FinCEN may issue **Section 314(a) requests** — FIIs must search their records and report matches within **14 days**, and must keep the existence of the request confidential (disclosing it can itself be an offence). Section 314(b) allows voluntary information sharing between institutions for AML purposes.
- **India:** the enforcement directorate and other agencies can issue summonses under **PMLA section 50** compelling production of records and attendance for examination.
- **EU:** member-state procedures vary; Europol/Eurojust channels and EU production orders (where implemented) apply to cross-border cases.

## Responding to requests

When a request arrives:

1. **Verify authenticity** — confirm the request is genuine, from the correct authority, with proper legal basis. For US 314(a) requests this means verifying through the FinCEN gateway, not email.
2. **Assess scope** — what exactly is being requested? Is it specific or broad?
3. **Check legal basis** — does the request have the required legal authority (warrant, order, statutory power)?
4. **Consult compliance/legal** — do not respond unilaterally; involve the MLRO and legal team.
5. **Respond within deadline** — production orders and 314(a) requests have strict timelines (14 days for 314(a)).
6. **Document everything** — what was requested, what was provided, when, and under what authority.

## Production orders specifically

A production order is a court order compelling the institution to produce specified documents or information. Key points:

- **Scope** — the order specifies exactly what must be produced; do not volunteer more.
- **Timeline** — production orders have strict deadlines; failure to comply is contempt of court (UK) or punishable non-compliance (US).
- **Format** — documents may need to be produced in specific formats or certified.
- **Privilege** — legal professional privilege may protect some communications; consult legal before producing.
- **SAR content** — internal SARs are generally not disclosable to the subject, but may be shared with the FIU/law enforcement as the regime permits; check whether the request covers SARs themselves before producing.

## Tipping-off in this context

The tipping-off prohibition continues to apply even when law enforcement is involved. You cannot tell the customer that law enforcement has requested information or that their account has been frozen — unless the law enforcement request itself authorises disclosure. In the US, Section 314(a) searches must be kept confidential; in the UK POCA tipping-off offences apply throughout.

## International cooperation

Cross-border requests add complexity:

- **MLATs** — formal treaties between countries for legal assistance.
- **FIU-to-FIU** — financial intelligence units share information directly in some cases (the Egmont Group framework).
- **Data protection** — GDPR and similar laws restrict cross-border data transfers; legal basis is required.
- **Conflicts of law** — a request lawful in one jurisdiction may conflict with blocking statutes or data-protection law in another; escalate rather than guess.

## The compliance team's role

The compliance function is the interface between the institution and law enforcement:

- Receive and verify requests.
- Assess legal basis and scope.
- Coordinate with legal, MLRO and business units.
- Ensure timely, accurate response.
- Maintain records of all requests and responses.
- Ensure no tipping-off occurs during the process.

## Takeaways

- Requests: information requests, production orders, freezes/restraints, warrants, international MLATs.
- UK freezes normally flow from a refused DAML or court restraint order — not a police request.
- US: grand jury subpoenas and FinCEN **314(a) requests (14-day response, confidentiality required)**; 314(b) allows voluntary sharing.
- India: **PMLA s.50 summonses**; UK: PACE/POCA instruments.
- Verify authenticity, assess scope, check legal basis, consult compliance/legal, respond on time, document.
- Production orders have strict deadlines and specific scope — do not over-produce.
- Tipping-off prohibition continues — customer cannot be told about law enforcement involvement.
