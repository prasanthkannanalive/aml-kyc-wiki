# Customer risk rating in practice: tiers, triggers and overrides

The risk rating is where the risk-based approach becomes operational. A score on a screen is not a control — a rating that changes what the firm does is. This chapter covers how ratings are structured, what should trigger a re-rate, and how overrides are controlled.

## Building the tier structure

Most programmes use 3–5 tiers. A common structure:

- **Low** — standard CDD, standard monitoring, longest review cycle.
- **Medium** — standard CDD with some additional checks, standard monitoring with some tuning, medium review cycle.
- **High** — EDD, senior approval, enhanced monitoring rules, annual or more frequent review.
- **Prohibited/Exit** — customers the firm will not serve or must exit.

Each tier maps to a control profile that is documented and applied consistently. If two customers have the same rating, they should receive the same control depth — inconsistency is what examiners flag.

## What triggers a re-rate

Ratings must move when the underlying risk moves:

- **Automatic triggers** — sanctions hit, PEP status confirmed, adverse media, document expiry.
- **Event triggers** — ownership change, new product, new corridor, unusual activity escalation.
- **Time triggers** — the periodic review cycle itself.
- **Analyst triggers** — an investigator or reviewer identifies risk the model missed.

A customer rated low at onboarding who becomes a PEP two years later must be re-rated — if the system does not catch it, the file is stale.

## Overrides: the control that matters most

Overrides are where risk ratings are manually changed — up or down. They are necessary (models miss things) and dangerous (they can be used to suppress risk).

Override controls that work:

- **Documented rationale** — every override needs a written reason, not just a new score.
- **Segregation of duties** — the person overriding should not be the person benefiting from the lower rating.
- **Approval thresholds** — downgrades from high to low typically need senior sign-off.
- **Audit trail** — who overrode, when, why, and what happened to the customer's controls afterwards.
- **Review** — overrides should be sampled in QA like any other decision.

An override that moves a customer from high to low without changing their actual risk profile is the single most common rating failure examiners find.

## Rating quality indicators

Track: distribution across tiers (a portfolio that is 95% low-risk is a red flag), override rates, re-rate frequency, and whether high-rated customers actually receive EDD. The rating system's outputs should drive visible differences in control depth — that is what "risk-based" means in practice.

## Takeaways

- Tiers map to documented control profiles; same rating = same controls.
- Re-rates trigger on sanctions, PEP, adverse media, ownership change, product change and review cycles.
- Overrides need rationale, segregation, approval thresholds and audit trail.
- Track tier distribution, override rates and whether ratings actually change control depth.
