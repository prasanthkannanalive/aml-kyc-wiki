# Requests for Information: best practices

The Request for Information — RFI — is how the investigation team asks the customer (or a third party) for the documents and explanations needed to resolve an alert. It is the most common customer-facing touchpoint in the AML process, and it is where investigations most often stall or fail.

## What an RFI should contain

A good RFI is specific, proportionate and clear:

- **The specific transactions or activity** being questioned — dates, amounts, counterparties.
- **The specific information needed** — source of funds documentation, invoices, contracts, explanation of purpose.
- **The deadline** — a clear response date, with consequences for non-response stated.
- **The format** — what documents are acceptable, how to submit them.
- **The confidentiality notice** — without tipping off, remind the customer that information provided will be used for compliance purposes.

## RFI best practices

The Wolfsberg RFI guidance emphasises several principles:

- **Be specific** — "please explain the transactions on 15 March" is better than "please explain this activity."
- **Be proportionate** — request only what is needed to resolve the alert, not a full KYC refresh unless required.
- **Be clear about format** — accept standard documents (bank statements, invoices, contracts) but specify what you need.
- **Set reasonable deadlines** — enough time for the customer to gather documents, not so long that the investigation stalls.
- **Follow up** — track responses, escalate non-responses, and document every interaction.

## What not to do

- **Do not tip off** — never hint that the customer is under investigation for money laundering. RFIs should be framed as routine compliance requests.
- **Do not over-request** — asking for everything makes it harder for the customer to respond and easier for them to miss something.
- **Do not accept vague answers** — "business purposes" is not an explanation; push for specifics.
- **Do not ignore non-response** — a customer who does not respond is itself a risk signal; document it and escalate.

## Handling responses

When the customer responds:

- **Verify the documents** — check authenticity, dates, consistency with the transactions.
- **Cross-check against the file** — does the explanation match the KYC profile and transaction history?
- **Assess sufficiency** — does the response actually resolve the alert, or does it raise new questions?
- **Document the assessment** — what was provided, what was verified, what was concluded.

## The escalation path

If the customer does not respond, responds inadequately, or the response raises new concerns:

1. Send a follow-up RFI with a firmer deadline.
2. Escalate to L2 or compliance if still unresolved.
3. Consider whether the account should be restricted or exited.
4. If suspicion remains, file the SAR/STR — non-response can itself be part of the suspicious activity narrative.

## Takeaways

- RFI = specific transactions + specific information needed + clear deadline + format + confidentiality.
- Be specific, proportionate, clear; never tip off.
- Verify responses against the file — documents, explanations, consistency.
- Non-response is a risk signal — document it and escalate.
- Inadequate response → follow-up → escalation → possible restriction/exit → possible SAR.
