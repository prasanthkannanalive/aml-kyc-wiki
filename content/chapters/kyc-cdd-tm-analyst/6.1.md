# SAR/STR fundamentals and the Five Ws + How

The suspicious activity report — SAR in the US, STR in the UK, EU and most other jurisdictions — is the output of the entire AML programme. It is the only document that leaves the institution and tells the financial intelligence unit what you found. Understanding what it is, when it is required, and what it must contain is fundamental.

## What triggers a report

The standard across jurisdictions is knowledge or suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing — not proof. The threshold is:

- **Knowledge** — you know the activity is suspicious.
- **Suspicion** — you have a reasonable suspicion based on the facts, even if you cannot prove it.

The bar is suspicion, not certainty. Waiting for proof means waiting too long — by the time you can prove it, the money has moved and the opportunity to report has been wasted.

## The Five Ws + How

Every SAR/STR must answer:

- **Who** — the customer(s) involved, their identifiers, their relationship to the institution.
- **What** — the specific suspicious activity: transactions, amounts, counterparties, channels.
- **When** — the dates and time period covered.
- **Where** — the jurisdictions involved, the locations of accounts and transactions.
- **Why suspicious** — the facts that give rise to suspicion, not just a label.
- **How** — how the institution became aware, what checks were performed, what was found.

The "why" is the hardest and most important — it is where the narrative lives.

## Jurisdictional variations

The core obligation is universal, but the details differ:

- **US FinCEN** — SAR filed within 30 days of initial detection; CTR for cash over $10,000.
- **UK NCA** — SAR submitted as soon as practicable; no fixed deadline but "promptly" is expected.
- **EU FIUs** — deadlines vary by member state, typically days not weeks.
- **FIU-IND** — STR filed within prescribed timeframes; specific formats for different entity types.

Always check your jurisdiction's specific requirements — the principle is universal, the mechanics are local.

## What a SAR is not

- It is not an accusation — it reports suspicion, not guilt.
- It is not a customer notification — tipping off is prohibited.
- It is not optional — failure to report when required is itself an offence.
- It is not the end of the process — law enforcement may follow up with requests for information or production orders.

## The reporting decision

Deciding whether to file is not the analyst's alone — it typically involves MLRO or compliance sign-off. But the analyst's investigation determines whether the facts support suspicion. A weak investigation produces weak SARs; a thorough one produces reports that can actually be acted on.

## Takeaways

- SAR/STR is required on knowledge or suspicion — not proof.
- The Five Ws + How: who, what, when, where, why suspicious, how detected.
- Deadlines vary by jurisdiction — know yours.
- SAR is not accusation, notification, optional, or the end of the process.
- The reporting decision is shared; the investigation quality determines the report quality.
