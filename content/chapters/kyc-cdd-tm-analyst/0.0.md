# Introduction & how to use this guide

This guide is written for the analyst who sits between the customer and the regulator: the person who decides what a bank, fintech or payments firm must know about a customer before it serves them, watches what they do once onboarded, investigates what looks wrong, and writes the report when suspicion cannot be resolved.

It is not a law textbook. Jurisdictions differ — the US FinCEN rules, the UK FCA and MLR regime, EU AMLD transpositions and India's FIU-IND framework all share a common spine, but thresholds, deadlines and form names change. Where a rule is jurisdiction-specific, this guide says so and points you to the regulator. Where a principle is universal — verify the customer, understand the money, report what you cannot explain — it says that too.

## Who this guide is for

- **L1 onboarding and alert analysts** who run CDD checks, refresh KYC files and triage the first wave of transaction-monitoring alerts.
- **L2 investigators** who take escalated alerts and KYC exceptions, gather evidence, and decide whether the facts support suspicion.
- **QA and team leads** who review dispositions, tune monitoring rules and defend the programme to auditors and examiners.
- **Career movers** preparing for AML/KYC analyst interviews — the scenario prompts in the Interview Prep section are drawn from the same material.

## How the guide is organised

**Module 1 — The Risk-Based Approach & Customer Lifecycle** covers the organising principle of AML: how firms decide where risk sits, score it, and apply controls proportionately across the customer lifecycle — from onboarding through perpetual KYC to exit.

**Module 2 — Individual KYC & PEPs** covers verifying that a person is who they claim to be — documents, digital ID, liveness — and the enhanced due diligence owed to politically exposed persons, their relatives and close associates.

**Module 3 — Corporate KYC (KYB) & UBO** covers onboarding legal entities: registry verification, unwrapping holding structures, trusts and foundations, nominees and bearer shares, and the threshold maths of beneficial ownership.

**Module 4 — Transaction Monitoring Mechanics & Typologies** covers how monitoring systems work — rules vs behavioural models, thresholds and tuning — and the typologies they exist to catch: structuring, pass-through flows, funnel accounts and trade-based laundering.

**Module 5 — Alert Investigation & Evidence Gathering** covers the investigative craft: case workflow, open-source intelligence, adverse media assessment, counterparty analysis, RFIs and case documentation.

**Module 6 — SAR/STR Narrative Writing & Regulatory Reporting** covers the output of the whole process: what must be reported, how to write a narrative that survives review, jurisdictional deadlines, tipping-off, filing mechanics and law enforcement follow-up.

## Sources

This programme is grounded in primary regulatory and industry material — FATF Recommendations and guidance, Wolfsberg group statements, FinCEN advisories and guidance, the FFIEC BSA/AML manual, EBA risk-factor guidelines, Basel standards and FIU-IND rules. The full source list, organised by module, appears at the end of this course page.

## What you should already know

You should be comfortable with the basic vocabulary — KYC, CDD, EDD, STR/SAR, PEP, UBO. If those terms are new, start with the Glossary and the Foundations section of Interview Prep, then come back.

## How to study it

Read each chapter once for the concepts, then revisit it with the case studies in `/cases` and the discipline hubs in `/disciplines` open alongside — every failure in this field was somebody else's version of a chapter you are reading now. Each module ends with a five-question knowledge check. After all six modules, the course page includes a 10-question final assessment — pass mark 70%. Passing issues a course-completion badge recorded in your browser; it is a knowledge-base credential, not an external accreditation.

This guide teaches how compliance works in practice and is not legal advice. Always verify current requirements against your regulator's published rules.
