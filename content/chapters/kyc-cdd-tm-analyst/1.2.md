# Customer risk factors: geography, product and channel

Risk scoring is only as good as its factors. The three factor families that carry most of the weight in any AML risk model are geography, product and delivery channel — and each has specific, teachable red flags.

## Geographic risk

Where the customer is, where they operate, and where their money moves:

- **FATF lists** — black-listed jurisdictions (called for countermeasures) and grey-listed (increased monitoring) jurisdictions drive automatic rating uplifts.
- **National high-risk lists** — countries like the UK's HM Treasury list or EU high-risk third countries add jurisdiction-specific weight.
- **Conflict and corruption** — FATF and Basel guidance point to states with weak governance, high corruption perception, or active conflict as higher risk.
- **Corridors** — not just the customer's country, but the payment corridors they use. A UK company routinely paying a secrecy-jurisdiction entity inherits that counterparty's risk.

Geography is the easiest factor to automate — list feeds update it — and the easiest to get wrong when analysts treat "customer's country" as the only geography that matters.

## Product and service risk

What the customer will actually do:

- **Cash intensity** — businesses handling large cash volumes (MSBs, casinos, precious metals, some retailers) carry inherent placement risk.
- **Anonymity features** — prepaid products, certain crypto services, bearer instruments.
- **Cross-border reach** — international wires, trade finance, correspondent relationships multiply exposure.
- **Speed and reversibility** — instant payments and crypto rails reduce the window to intercept.
- **Complexity** — trade finance, correspondent banking, agent networks add layers where risk hides.

A product that is low-risk for one customer may be high-risk for another — a payments business serving high-risk corridors scores differently from a domestic payroll provider.

## Channel risk

How the customer is acquired and served:
- **Non-face-to-face onboarding** — remote channels lack the visual and contextual cues of branch KYC; they demand stronger document verification and liveness checks.
- **Third-party reliance** — depending on another institution's CDD shifts but does not remove risk.
- **Agent and intermediary networks** — customers brought in by agents you cannot fully assess inherit the agent's risk.
- **Introductions and referrals** — a warm introduction from a trusted correspondent is still a customer you must verify yourself.

## Combining factors

No single factor decides the rating — the model weighs them together. A domestic customer (low geo) using a high-risk product (high product) over a remote channel (medium channel) can still land medium-high overall. Analysts should be able to explain a customer's rating factor-by-factor, not just quote the score.

## Takeaways

- Geography = customer location + corridors + FATF/national lists + corruption/conflict.
- Product risk = cash intensity, anonymity, cross-border reach, speed, complexity.
- Channel risk = remote onboarding, third-party reliance, agents, intermediaries.
- Ratings combine factors — be able to explain any score factor-by-factor.
