# OSINT techniques for AML investigations

Open-source intelligence — OSINT — is the analyst's research toolkit: publicly available information that can corroborate or contradict what the customer has told you. Used well, it transforms an alert from "the customer says X" into "here is what the public record actually shows."

## What OSINT covers

- **Corporate registries** — companies house filings, officers, shareholders, registered addresses, filing history.
- **Court and regulatory records** — lawsuits, enforcement actions, sanctions listings, insolvency proceedings.
- **News and media** — adverse media, press coverage, investigative journalism.
- **Social media and web presence** — professional profiles, business claims, lifestyle indicators.
- **Government databases** — sanctions lists, PEP databases, politically exposed persons registers, charity commissions.
- **Domain and web infrastructure** — website registration, hosting, content analysis.
- **Professional networks** — LinkedIn, business directories, industry associations.

## OSINT in practice

For a corporate customer, OSINT might reveal: the registered address is a virtual office, the company was incorporated last month, the sole director is a nominee, the website shows a different business than declared, or the "trading company" has no employees and no premises.

For an individual, OSINT might reveal: the declared employer does not exist, the person's online presence shows wealth inconsistent with declared income, or the individual is connected to sanctioned entities through professional networks.

## Methodology matters

OSINT is not googling — it is systematic:

1. **Define the question** — what specifically are you trying to verify or disprove?
2. **Identify sources** — which databases, registries, news sources are relevant?
3. **Collect and date-stamp** — record what you found, where, and when.
4. **Corroborate** — one source is a lead; two independent sources is evidence.
5. **Document** — every finding needs a citation: URL, date accessed, what it showed.

## Limitations and ethics

OSINT has limits: paywalled databases, languages you do not read, jurisdictions with poor online records, and the deliberate opacity of secrecy jurisdictions. It also has ethical boundaries — you are investigating for legitimate compliance purposes, not stalking. Stay within public sources and your firm's acceptable-use policy.

## The corroboration principle

OSINT findings should corroborate or contradict the customer's representations — not replace them. A customer who says "I run a successful import business" but whose company has no employees, no premises and no trade references has a corroboration problem that needs investigation, not assumption.

## Takeaways

- OSINT = corporate registries, court records, news, social media, government databases, web infrastructure.
- Method: define question → identify sources → collect + date-stamp → corroborate → document.
- One source is a lead; two independent sources is evidence.
- OSINT corroborates or contradicts customer claims — it does not replace verification.
- Stay within public sources and acceptable-use policy.
