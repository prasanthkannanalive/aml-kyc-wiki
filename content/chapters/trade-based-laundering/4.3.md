## 4.3 Escalation, reporting and the law-enforcement interface

TBML cases require coordination across multiple functions and often involve law enforcement. This chapter covers the escalation and reporting process.

### When to escalate

TBML alerts should be escalated when:

- **Multiple red flags** — several documentary, pricing or counterparty indicators present
- **High-value transactions** — large trade payments with suspicious characteristics
- **Sanctions exposure** — transactions involving sanctioned parties, jurisdictions or goods
- **Network indicators** — patterns suggesting TBML networks or shell company structures
- **Customer inability to explain** — customer cannot provide satisfactory explanation for trade anomalies

### Escalation paths

**Within the bank** — TBML alerts typically escalate through:

1. **Trade finance team** — for documentary review and trade-specific expertise
2. **Compliance/AML team** — for suspicious activity assessment and SAR filing
3. **Senior management** — for high-risk cases or potential law-enforcement referral
4. **External counsel** — for complex legal questions or potential litigation

**To law enforcement** — TBML cases may be referred to:

- **Financial intelligence units (FIUs)** — for suspicious activity reports
- **Customs authorities** — for trade fraud, tariff evasion, or sanctions violations
- **Police or prosecutor** — for criminal investigation of TBML networks
- **International organizations** — INTERPOL, Europol, or FATF for cross-border coordination

### What to include in escalation

**Transaction details** — dates, amounts, currencies, counterparties, payment methods.

**Documentary evidence** — copies of invoices, bills of lading, certificates of origin, and other trade documents.

**Analysis** — the specific red flags identified, market-price comparisons, counterparty research, and network analysis.

**Customer context** — customer profile, business model, transaction history, and any explanations provided.

**Recommended action** — whether to file SAR, restrict transactions, terminate relationship, or refer to law enforcement.

### SAR filing for TBML

TBML SARs require specific elements:

- **Clear narrative** — explain the trade transaction, the red flags, and why they suggest TBML
- **Documentary evidence** — reference specific documents and inconsistencies
- **Market context** — include pricing benchmarks or market comparisons where available
- **Counterparty information** — include corporate registry data, beneficial ownership, and web presence findings
- **Pattern analysis** — describe how the transaction fits into a broader pattern of suspicious activity

### Law-enforcement cooperation

When TBML cases are referred to law enforcement:

- **Provide all relevant documents** — trade documents, customer records, transaction histories
- **Explain the technical aspects** — TBML techniques, red flags, and detection methods
- **Cooperate with investigation** — provide additional information as requested
- **Maintain confidentiality** — do not tip off customers or counterparties

### Cross-border coordination

TBML is inherently cross-border. Cases may involve:

- **Multiple jurisdictions** — buyer, seller, banks, and shipping companies in different countries
- **International law enforcement** — INTERPOL, Europol, or bilateral police cooperation
- **FIU-to-FIU exchange** — sharing suspicious activity intelligence through Egmont Group channels
- **Regulatory cooperation** — sharing information with foreign regulators or supervisors

### The confidentiality challenge

TBML investigations often involve multiple parties across jurisdictions. Maintaining confidentiality while coordinating is challenging:

- **Tipping-off risk** — informing customers or counterparties may compromise investigations
- **Cross-border information sharing** — sharing data across jurisdictions requires legal authority
- **Document preservation** — ensuring trade documents are preserved for investigation
- **Witness protection** — protecting sources and cooperating witnesses

## What to take from this chapter

- **Multiple red flags** — several documentary, pricing or counterparty indicators present
- **High-value transactions** — large trade payments with suspicious characteristics
- **Sanctions exposure** — transactions involving sanctioned parties, jurisdictions or goods
- **Network indicators** — patterns suggesting TBML networks or shell company structures
- **Customer inability to explain** — customer cannot provide satisfactory explanation for trade anomalies
- **Trade finance team** — for documentary review and trade-specific expertise
