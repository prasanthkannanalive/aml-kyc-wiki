## 2.3 Misdescription, services and TBML in invoices

Misdescription and services TBML are subtler techniques that exploit gaps in how goods and services are documented and valued.

### Misdescription — the wrong label

**Mechanism** — goods are described inaccurately on invoices and shipping documents to evade tariffs, sanctions, inspection or simply to obscure their true nature.

**Types of misdescription**:

**Tariff evasion** — high-tariff goods declared as low-tariff alternatives. Electronics declared as "computer parts"; luxury goods declared as "gifts"; alcohol declared as "food products".

**Sanctions evasion** — sanctioned goods disguised as permitted alternatives. Military equipment declared as "industrial machinery"; dual-use goods declared as civilian products.

**Inspection evasion** — goods that would fail inspection declared as different products. Contaminated food declared as clean; counterfeit goods declared as genuine.

**Value misdescription** — describing goods inaccurately to support over- or under-invoicing. High-value goods declared as low-value components; services declared as goods.

**Detection** — misdescription is difficult to detect from documents alone because the documents are internally consistent. Red flags include:
- Descriptions that don't match the quantity or packaging
- Goods described in vague or generic terms ("miscellaneous items", "parts")
- Descriptions that don't match the customer's usual product lines
- Inconsistencies between invoice descriptions and shipping documents

### Services TBML — no goods required

Services TBML uses **intangible services** to launder money — no physical goods move, only invoices for services that may or may not have been rendered.

**Common service types**:

**Consulting fees** — Company A pays Company B $500,000 for "management consulting services". No deliverable is produced; the payment is the laundered value.

**Technical services** — "software development", "engineering services", "training services" — services that are difficult to verify and easy to inflate.

**Intellectual property** — royalties for patents, trademarks, copyrights — payments that can be inflated beyond market rates.

**Commission payments** — "sales commissions", "agent fees", "brokerage fees" — payments for services that may not have been performed.

**Detection** — services TBML is harder to detect than goods TBML because:
- There are no shipping documents to verify
- Services are inherently intangible — you cannot inspect them
- Market prices for services are often opaque
- The "delivery" of services is not independently verifiable

**Red flags**:
- Service invoices from counterparties with no verifiable business presence
- Services that don't match the customer's industry or capabilities
- Large service payments with no corresponding deliverables
- Services invoiced but not evidenced in contracts or reports
- Services paid to shell companies or recently-incorporated entities

### The combined approach

TBML often combines multiple techniques: goods are misdescribed to support over-invoicing, phantom shipments are documented with service invoices, or services are used to launder value that would be too visible in goods trade.

The analyst must look at the **entire transaction** — not just the invoice, but the shipment, the payment, the counterparty and the pattern across transactions.
