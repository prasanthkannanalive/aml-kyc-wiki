## 4.4 Building a TBML control programme

This chapter synthesizes the guide into a practical framework for building and maintaining a TBML control programme.

### Risk assessment

**Identify TBML exposure** — assess which products, customers and corridors are most exposed to TBML risk:

- Trade finance products (documentary credits, open account, collections)
- Corporate customers in trade-intensive industries
- High-risk jurisdictions and trade corridors
- Customers with complex supply chains or international operations

**Assess inherent risk** — evaluate the risk factors:

- Customer risk (industry, geography, ownership structure)
- Product risk (trade finance complexity, document types)
- Geographic risk (jurisdictions, sanctions exposure)
- Transaction risk (volumes, values, frequencies)

**Determine risk appetite** — define the level of TBML risk the institution is willing to accept and the controls needed to mitigate it.

### Policy and procedures

**TBML policy** — establish clear policies covering:

- Scope (which products and customers are covered)
- Risk assessment methodology
- Due diligence requirements
- Monitoring and detection procedures
- Escalation and reporting processes
- Training and awareness requirements

**Trade finance procedures** — integrate TBML controls into trade finance operations:

- Document review requirements
- Pricing verification procedures
- Counterparty due diligence
- Enhanced due diligence triggers
- Suspicious activity escalation paths

### Monitoring and detection

**Deploy TBML monitoring rules** — implement the rule categories from Chapter 3.4:

- Pricing deviation rules
- Documentation inconsistency rules
- Counterparty risk rules
- Network pattern rules
- Geographic anomaly rules

**Integrate trade data** — ensure trade documents and data are available to monitoring systems:

- Invoice data extraction and storage
- Bill of lading tracking
- Certificate of origin verification
- Market pricing data integration

**Tune and test** — regularly review rule effectiveness:

- False positive analysis
- Alert outcome tracking
- Rule threshold optimization
- Regulatory feedback incorporation

### Training and awareness

**Role-based training** — different training for different roles:

- **Trade finance staff** — document review, red flag recognition, escalation procedures
- **AML analysts** — TBML typologies, monitoring rules, investigation techniques
- **Compliance officers** — policy, regulatory requirements, program oversight
- **Senior management** — risk appetite, program effectiveness, regulatory expectations

**Ongoing awareness** — keep staff current on:

- Emerging TBML techniques
- Regulatory guidance updates
- Industry typologies and case studies
- Internal program changes

### Testing and audit

**Regular testing** — test TBML controls against:

- Known TBML scenarios
- Historical cases
- Regulatory examination scenarios
- Red team exercises

**Internal audit** — independent review of:

- Control design and implementation
- Rule effectiveness and tuning
- Staff competency and training
- Documentation and record-keeping

**Regulatory examination readiness** — maintain evidence of:

- Risk assessments
- Policy and procedure documentation
- Training records
- Monitoring and alert data
- Investigation and escalation records
- SAR filings and outcomes

### Continuous improvement

**Monitor effectiveness** — track key metrics:

- Alert volumes and false positive rates
- SAR filing rates and outcomes
- Customer risk rating accuracy
- Regulatory examination findings
- Industry typology developments

**Update controls** — adjust monitoring rules, procedures and training based on:

- Emerging TBML techniques
- Regulatory guidance changes
- Internal audit findings
- Industry best practices

**Benchmark** — compare program effectiveness against industry peers and regulatory expectations.

### The ultimate goal

A TBML control programme is not about eliminating all TBML risk — that is impossible. It is about:

- **Detecting** TBML when it occurs
- **Reporting** suspicious activity to authorities
- **Deterring** TBML through visible controls
- **Protecting** the institution from regulatory and reputational risk
- **Contributing** to the global fight against financial crime

The programme must be **risk-based** (proportionate to actual risk), **evidence-based** (supported by data and analysis), and **adaptive** (evolving with the threat landscape).
