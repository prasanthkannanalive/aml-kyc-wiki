# Trade-Based Money Laundering — Study Guide

This guide is the course material for the Trade-Based Money Laundering Analyst credential. It is educational material only — not legal advice — and it does not state the law of any jurisdiction as it applies to any particular transaction. TBML typologies evolve with trade practices and sanctions regimes; verify current FATF guidance and your institution's procedures before relying on anything here.

## Who this guide is for

- **Trade finance and correspondent banking analysts** who review documentary credits, open-account transactions and trade payments.
- **AML analysts covering corporate clients** whose customers import or export goods or services.
- **Sanctions and dual-use compliance staff** who need to understand how trade documents can be manipulated.
- **Risk and audit teams** building or testing TBML controls.

## Why TBML deserves its own course

TBML is the largest single channel for moving illicit value across borders — and the hardest for banks to detect. The bank sees documents and payments; the crime lives in whether the goods actually moved, whether the price was real, and whether the parties are who they claim to be. A wire transfer has a clear originator and beneficiary; a trade transaction has invoices, bills of lading, certificates of origin and inspection reports — any of which can be forged, manipulated or simply false.

## How to use it

Module 1 establishes what TBML is and why trade is attractive. Module 2 covers the techniques in detail. Module 3 is the detection core — red flags and monitoring rules. Module 4 addresses correspondent exposure and how to build a control programme.

## The one idea to hold onto

In TBML, **the document is the crime scene**. Unlike cash smuggling or wire fraud, the evidence is paper — invoices that don't match shipments, prices that don't match markets, counterparties that don't match reality. Your job is to read the documents critically and ask whether the trade they describe actually happened.
