## 3.1 Documentary red flags

The documents are the primary evidence in TBML — and the primary target for manipulation. This chapter covers the documentary red flags that should trigger review.

### Invoice red flags

**Inconsistent pricing** — prices that don't match market rates, round-number prices, or prices that change dramatically between orders for identical goods.

**Missing or incomplete information** — invoices without tax IDs, without detailed descriptions, without quantities or units, or with vague descriptions ("miscellaneous items", "parts").

**Inconsistencies between documents** — invoice amounts that don't match packing lists, descriptions that don't match bills of lading, dates that don't align.

**Unusual payment terms** — payment required before shipment, payment to third parties not named on invoices, or payment terms that don't match industry norms.

### Bill of lading red flags

**Missing or invalid B/L numbers** — bills of lading without verifiable tracking numbers or with numbers that don't match shipping company records.

**Inconsistencies with invoices** — B/L descriptions that don't match invoice descriptions, quantities that don't align, or dates that are impossible (shipped before ordered).

**Suspicious shipping companies** — bills of lading from obscure, recently-registered or sanctioned shipping companies.

**Missing endorsements** — bills of lading without proper endorsements or with endorsements that don't match the parties.

### Certificate of origin red flags

**Missing or invalid certificates** — certificates of origin that are photocopied, lack required stamps, or come from non-existent or unauthorized chambers of commerce.

**Inconsistencies with other documents** — certificates that describe different goods, quantities or destinations than invoices or bills of lading.

**Suspicious issuing authorities** — certificates from chambers of commerce in high-risk jurisdictions or from recently-established authorities.

### Inspection and certification red flags

**Missing inspection reports** — goods that should require inspection (food, pharmaceuticals, chemicals) but lack inspection certificates.

**Inspection reports from unknown firms** — reports from inspection companies that cannot be verified or that have no track record.

**Inconsistencies between inspection and shipping documents** — inspection reports that describe different goods or quantities than shipping documents.

### Cross-document inconsistencies

The most powerful documentary red flag is **inconsistency across documents**:

- Invoice says "500 units of steel pipes" but bill of lading says "500 metric tons of steel products"
- Commercial invoice dated January 15 but bill of lading dated January 10 (shipped before invoiced)
- Certificate of origin says goods are from Country A but bill of lading shows shipment from Country B
- Packing list shows different quantities than commercial invoice

Any single inconsistency may be a clerical error; **multiple inconsistencies across multiple documents** are a strong TBML indicator.
