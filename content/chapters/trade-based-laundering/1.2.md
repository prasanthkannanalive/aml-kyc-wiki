## 1.2 Why trade is the perfect laundering channel

Trade offers money launderers capabilities that no other channel provides: legitimate cover, global reach, documentary complexity, and exploitation of information asymmetries.

### Legitimate cover

A trade transaction comes with invoices, bills of lading, certificates of origin, inspection reports and letters of credit — an entire paper trail that looks like normal commerce. Banks process trade payments every day; a $500,000 payment for "industrial machinery" attracts far less scrutiny than a $500,000 wire to an unknown individual.

The documentation creates **plausible deniability**. Even if a bank suspects the trade is fictitious, proving it requires verifying that goods actually moved — which banks cannot do directly. They rely on the documents, and the documents can be forged.

### Global reach

Trade crosses borders by definition. A launderer can move value from a high-risk jurisdiction to a low-risk one through a series of "exports" and "imports" that never physically touch the launderer's home country. The goods may ship through multiple ports, change hands several times, or never leave the warehouse — but the invoices create a paper trail suggesting legitimate international commerce.

### Documentary complexity

A single trade transaction may involve dozens of documents: commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, certificates of origin, phytosanitary certificates, insurance documents, inspection reports, customs declarations. Each document is a potential point of manipulation — and banks rarely verify them all. They check for presence and apparent authenticity, not accuracy.

### Information asymmetry

The bank sees the documents the customer provides. The bank does not see: whether the goods actually exist, whether they shipped, whether they arrived, whether the price reflects market value, or whether the counterparty is a shell company. This information asymmetry is the foundation of TBML.

### Multiple techniques in one transaction

TBML is not a single technique but a family of methods that can be combined: over-invoicing to move value out, under-invoicing to move value in, phantom shipments to create false trade, multiple invoicing to multiply the same goods' value, misdescription to evade tariffs or sanctions, and services TBML to launder through consulting fees. Each technique exploits a different weakness in the trade finance system.
