## 3.2 Pricing and valuation red flags

Price is the value transfer mechanism in TBML — manipulating it is the core technique. This chapter covers the pricing red flags that signal manipulation.

### Market-price deviations

**Significant deviations from market rates** — prices that are 2x, 5x or 10x market value in either direction. For standardized commodities (oil, grain, metals), market prices are well-known; deviations are strong indicators.

**Gradual price drift** — prices that increase or decrease over time without market explanation. A customer who consistently pays 20% above market for "consulting services" is a red flag.

**Round-number pricing** — prices that are suspiciously round ($100,000, $500,000, $1,000,000) rather than negotiated amounts ($97,500, $487,200). Real trade involves negotiation; TBML often uses round numbers for simplicity.

### Quantity-price mismatches

**Prices that don't scale with quantity** — unit prices that don't decrease with volume (economies of scale) or that increase without explanation.

**Prices inconsistent with product specifications** — prices for "premium grade" that match "standard grade" market prices, or vice versa.

**Prices inconsistent with packaging** — prices that don't match the quantity or packaging described.

### Currency and payment red flags

**Invoicing in unusual currencies** — invoicing in currencies that don't match the trade corridor or that have recently appreciated/depreciated.

**Payments that don't match invoice amounts** — payments that are more or less than the invoice total, with the difference unexplained.

**Payments to third parties** — payments made to entities not named on the invoice or contract.

**Multiple payments for single invoices** — payments split across multiple accounts or jurisdictions to obscure the total.

### Historical pattern analysis

**New customers with large first orders** — customers who place unusually large orders immediately after onboarding, especially for high-value goods.

**Price changes between orders** — the same customer paying dramatically different prices for identical goods over time.

**Customer-specific pricing** — prices that are consistently higher or lower for specific customers compared to market or peers.

**Seasonal anomalies** — prices that don't match seasonal patterns or market conditions.

### The benchmarking challenge

The core difficulty is establishing **market price** for the specific goods or services. For standardized commodities, market data is available. For specialized goods or services, market prices are opaque.

**Practical approaches**:

- **Industry databases** — trade data providers, industry associations, pricing benchmarks
- **Peer comparison** — comparing prices across customers for similar goods
- **Historical analysis** — comparing prices over time for the same customer and product
- **Expert consultation** — industry experts who understand the specific market
- **Red flags, not proof** — pricing anomalies are indicators for review, not proof of TBML; legitimate reasons for price variation exist
